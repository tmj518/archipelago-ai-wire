The monthly cost of employing a foreign worker in China is made from separate parts: salary, employer social insurance, and the amortised cost of the permit. Because social insurance rules and caps differ by city, the same arrangement can produce different costs in different places. Treat any result as an estimate to be rebuilt for the relevant city.

## Cost parts to include

Salary is the amount the employer decides to pay; it is not taken from a published rate.
Employer social insurance contributions are a percentage of a declared salary base, and that base is capped according to the city.
The permit and its renewals are recurring costs rather than a charge paid only at hiring. Spread their cost across the covered months to show the monthly permit cost.

## City-specific calculation

Do not rely on a universal national total. Confirm the local contribution percentage, salary-base cap, and permit charging rules for the city where the employee will work.
Because these rules can change, the output is an estimate rather than a fixed price. Recalculate it for each relevant city and whenever the underlying inputs change.

## Result to produce

Add the decided salary, employer social insurance contribution, and amortised permit cost. The result is the estimated monthly employment cost per employee in the selected city.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-10-25; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
