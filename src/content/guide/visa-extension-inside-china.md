A visa can be extended, changed, or reissued inside China, but only through the exit-entry authority with jurisdiction over where you are. You must apply before your current stay expires; an overstay is a separate offence with its own penalty. Accepted extension reasons form a closed list, so a personal preference that is not on that list is not enough.

## Where to apply

Submit the application to the exit-entry authority that has jurisdiction over your place of stay. Another office cannot accept it merely because it is closer or more convenient.

## When and why to apply

Apply before the stay shown on your current visa or permit ends. An overstay is a separate offence with its own penalty, and applying late does not automatically remove that issue.
The reasons that justify an extension are a closed list. Your situation must fit an accepted reason; you cannot rely on any reason you prefer.

## Stay permit versus residence permit

A stay permit is a different document from a residence permit. Do not treat them as interchangeable or assume that a visa extension gives you residence status.
Ask the authority which document applies to your case, then check that document separately for its type and validity dates.

## Listed fees for an application

The following visa fee items are listed on www.nia.gov.cn, per person: zero-entry visa 160 元; one-entry visa for non-reciprocal countries 206 元; two-entry visa for non-reciprocal countries 313 元; half-year multiple-entry visa for non-reciprocal countries 413 元; one-year multiple-entry or longer visa for non-reciprocal countries 619 元.
Other listed items are: group visa separation for non-reciprocal countries 160 元 per person; adding or reducing accompanying persons for non-reciprocal countries 160 元 per person; foreigner residence permit change 200 元 per time.
Ask which fee item applies to your extension, change, or reissue before paying.

## Confirm before you act

Fees and processing times change. This page was checked on 2026-10-22; confirm the current rule with the authority named in each source above before you rely on a deadline.
