## AI Summary

Malaysia's foreign minister called on 14 November 2026 for Asean to implement its Cybersecurity Cooperation Strategy for 2026–2030, and welcomed a 2026–2028 work programme on transnational crime sitting under a 2026–2035 plan of action. The call came at the Asean political-security community council meeting ahead of the 49th Asean Summit. For Malaysian households, the test is whether the promised intelligence-sharing and border cooperation reaches the scam calls, fake job offers and smuggled goods that arrive on their phones and at their borders.

## What happened

Malaysia's foreign minister, Mohamad Hasan, used his intervention at the Asean political-security community council meeting on 14 November 2026 to call for the timely implementation of the Asean Cybersecurity Cooperation Strategy for 2026–2030. Free Malaysia Today reported the intervention on the same day, citing Bernama.

The strategy, as the minister described it, exists to address cyber threats, respond to incidents and exchange information among member states. Those are the three functions he named. The document itself was not published alongside the report; the description comes from the minister's remarks.

In the same intervention he welcomed the adoption of Asean's 2026–2028 work programme on transnational crime. That work programme supports a longer plan of action running from 2026 to 2035. The meeting took place ahead of the 49th Asean Summit and Related Summits in November 2026.

The minister's line, as reported, was that "Asean should not be the epicentre of transnational crime."

> **Transnational crime:** criminal activity planned in one country and carried out in another, or across several at once. Online scam operations run from call centres in one state against victims in another are the clearest example in Southeast Asia. Smuggling of goods and people across land and sea borders is another. The crime crosses a border; the police force that investigates it usually does not.

The same meeting covered two other items. The minister said nearly 90% of the proposed Code of Conduct in the South China Sea had been agreed by Asean and China. He also said Timor-Leste will chair Asean in 2029, following Thailand, under the bloc's alphabetical rotation.

## Why now

Three threads meet at this meeting.

The first is the calendar. Asean's cybersecurity strategy begins its run in 2026, and the transnational crime work programme covers 2026 to 2028. Both are new instruments. A minister calling for their implementation in November 2026 is calling for delivery at the start of the period, not at the end of it. The 49th Summit gives the call a stage.

The second is the shape of the crime itself. Scam operations in Southeast Asia are organised across borders: a compound in one country, recruited workers from several others, victims drawn from the region and beyond. A police report filed in Malaysia names a bank account and a phone number. The operation behind it sits in another jurisdiction. That mismatch is the reason a regional instrument exists at all.

The third is the pattern of Asean security work. The bloc has spent years building plans of action that run for a decade and work programmes that run for two or three years inside them. The 2026–2028 work programme sitting under the 2026–2035 plan of action is that structure in its current form. The cybersecurity strategy follows the same rhythm.

What surfaced this month is not a new problem. It is the point at which the new instruments start to be measured against the crime they were written for.

## So what

The judgement is about where the binding constraint sits. A regional strategy does not arrest anyone. It sets what member states agree to share, how fast they share it, and through which channel. The gain from a working strategy lands on the country whose police force can act on a tip that arrives from another capital. The cost of a weak one lands on the victim whose report stops at the border.

The mechanism is information. A scam network leaves traces in several countries at once: a phone number registered in one, a bank account in another, a server in a third, workers recruited from a fourth. Each national police force holds one piece. The value of a regional strategy is that the pieces meet. The delay is the negotiation over what each state is willing to hand over, and how quickly, and to whom.

The second actor is the smuggler. Border cooperation is slower to build than a data channel because it involves physical checkpoints, vessel patrols and customs officers on both sides of a line. A work programme that runs to 2028 sets a horizon for that cooperation. It does not set a seizure target.

The third actor is the firm that operates the infrastructure. Banks, telcos and platforms hold the account records and call logs that investigations need. A regional strategy that stops at government-to-government sharing leaves the private records outside the channel. Whether the strategy reaches them is not stated in the report.

## What it means for Malaysia

The consequence lands on the Malaysian household that receives the scam call, and on the small firm at the border town that buys stock from a supplier across the line.

Take the household first. A Malaysian who loses money to an online scam files a police report. The report names a phone number and a bank account. If the operation behind it sits outside Malaysia, the investigation needs a request to another country's authorities. The 2026–2030 strategy names information exchange among member states as one of its functions. The concrete carrier is that request: how long it takes, and whether it is answered. A household does not see the strategy. It sees whether the money comes back.

Take the small firm second. A trader in a border town who buys goods that turn out to be smuggled carries the loss when the goods are seized. The 2026–2028 work programme on transnational crime covers the cooperation between enforcement agencies that determines how much smuggled stock moves through a crossing. The carrier is the checkpoint and the customs officer, not the summit.

Take the worker third. Scam compounds in the region recruit workers, some of them Malaysian, with fake job offers. The same information exchange that traces a victim's money traces a recruiter's trail. The carrier is the consular case and the police report filed on behalf of a family member abroad.

The honest limit: the report names the instruments and the minister's call. It does not name a single operational outcome, a seizure, an arrest or a repatriation that the strategy has produced. A Malaysian reading this knows the plan exists. Whether it reaches the phone in their hand is the open question.

## For you

- **A household that has been scammed:** file the police report and keep the phone number and bank account details. The regional strategy's value to you is the speed of the request it sends abroad, so ask the investigating officer whether a cross-border request has been made.
- **A job seeker weighing an offer abroad:** treat an offer that arrives through a messaging app, asks you to travel first and describes the salary only after you arrive as a recruitment risk. The regional crime work programme covers the networks that run these offers; your protection is checking the employer before you travel.
- **A small trader near a border crossing:** keep invoices and customs paperwork for every consignment. If smuggled goods enter the same supply chain, the firm holding the paperwork is the one that keeps its stock.
