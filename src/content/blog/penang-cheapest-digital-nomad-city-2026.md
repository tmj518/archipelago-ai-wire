## What happened

Penang has been named the world's cheapest city for digital nomads in a study by Click Intelligence, a digital marketing agency, published in Moneywise. The study covered 26 cities across 15 countries and gave Penang an overall score of 0.78, placing it first and beating 25 other cities.

The study estimated Penang's monthly cost of living, including rent, at €850, which is approximately RM3,860. The ranking also considered internet connectivity, safety and access to Malaysia's DE Rantau Pass, a programme for eligible remote workers that allows them to stay in Malaysia for 12 months, with an option to renew.

> **Digital nomad:** a person who works remotely, usually online, and travels between locations rather than living in one fixed city. A digital nomad visa or pass is a formal arrangement that lets such workers stay legally in a country for an extended period.

The study is a ranking, not a government policy. It compares cities on cost and practical factors that matter to remote workers. Penang's position at the top is the headline, and the DE Rantau Pass is the mechanism that makes a long stay legal.

## Why now

The ranking surfaces this month because the study was published in Moneywise in October 2026. But the thread behind it is longer. Malaysia has been building the legal and digital infrastructure for remote work since the DE Rantau Pass programme was introduced, and the pass is now a settled feature of the immigration system.

The DE Rantau Pass is the concrete carrier. It gives eligible remote workers a 12-month stay with a renewal option, which is the legal basis for a digital nomad to live in Penang rather than visit for a week. Without the pass, a remote worker would be limited to a tourist visa and the spending that comes with a short trip.

The study's timing also reflects a broader shift. Cities around the world have been competing for remote workers since the pandemic normalised working from anywhere, and the competition has moved from simply offering visas to offering a full package of cost, connectivity and safety. Penang's ranking is a signal that Malaysia's package is competitive on price, which is the factor that most nomads weigh first.

## So what

The gain is a marketing asset for Malaysia, and it lands on specific businesses. A digital nomad who stays 12 months spends on rent, food, transport, co-working space and entertainment every month, not just for a holiday week. That recurring spending is what the tourism industry has been trying to capture, because a long-stay visitor is worth more than a short-stay one.

The cost side is the trade-off that comes with any ranking. A city that is cheap for a foreign remote worker is a city where local wages and prices are low by international standards. The same affordability that attracts a nomad with a euro or dollar salary is a constraint for a local worker earning in ringgit. The ranking does not resolve that tension; it exposes it.

The study also names the factors that matter beyond price. Internet connectivity, safety and the DE Rantau Pass are part of the score, which means Penang's ranking is not only about being cheap. A city that is cheap but unsafe or poorly connected would not rank first. The score of 0.78 bundles all of those factors into one number.

## What it means for Malaysia

The ranking lands on the tourism and creative-economy sectors in Penang and beyond. A hotel, a co-working space, a cafe or a design studio that serves remote workers now has an external validation to cite when marketing to international clients. The study is a third-party endorsement that a business can link to in its own materials.

The DE Rantau Pass is the operational carrier. A remote worker who wants to act on the ranking needs the pass to stay 12 months, and the pass is a federal programme, not a Penang one. That means the ranking benefits any Malaysian city that can offer similar cost and connectivity, and the pass is the common legal platform.

The spending pattern matters for local businesses. A digital nomad on a 12-month stay rents an apartment, buys groceries, uses transport and pays for services month after month. That is different from a tourist who stays three nights. The businesses that capture that recurring spending are the ones that market to long-stay visitors rather than short-stay ones.

The ranking also points to a risk. If Penang becomes known as a cheap base, the inflow of foreign remote workers can put upward pressure on rents in the neighbourhoods they favour. That is a scenario, not a fact, and the study does not measure it. Local stakeholders who want the benefit of the ranking should watch the rental market in the areas where nomads concentrate.

## FAQ

**Is the DE Rantau Pass the same as a digital nomad visa?**

The DE Rantau Pass is Malaysia's formal arrangement for remote workers, and it functions like a digital nomad visa in other countries. It allows eligible remote workers to stay in Malaysia for 12 months, with an option to renew. The pass is a federal programme, so it applies across Malaysia, not only in Penang. Eligibility is tied to working remotely for a foreign employer or running a foreign business, and applicants must meet income and other requirements set by the programme. The pass is the legal basis that turns a short tourist visit into a long stay, which is why the study cites it as a factor in Penang's ranking.

**Does the ranking mean Penang is cheap for Malaysians too?**

The ranking measures the cost of living for a digital nomad, which includes rent and daily expenses, and the €850 figure is an estimate for that group. Whether that translates to a low cost for a Malaysian household depends on income and spending patterns. A foreign remote worker earning in euros or dollars finds RM3,860 a month manageable. A local worker earning in ringgit faces the same prices with a different income. The ranking does not compare local wages, so it does not answer whether Penang is affordable for Malaysians.

**What should a business do with this ranking?**

A business that serves remote workers can use the study as a third-party endorsement. Link to the study in marketing materials, mention the 0.78 score and the €850 monthly cost, and highlight the 12-month DE Rantau Pass. The ranking is most useful for businesses that can serve long-stay visitors, such as co-working spaces, serviced apartments and cafes. A business that targets short-stay tourists will find the ranking less relevant, because the study is about monthly living costs, not holiday spending.
