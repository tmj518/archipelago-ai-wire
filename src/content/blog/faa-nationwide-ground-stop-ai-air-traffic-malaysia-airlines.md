## So what

The gain from a ground stop lands on safety. When departures stop, the system has time to resolve whatever condition triggered the order. Passengers on the ground wait; passengers in the air land. Nobody is exposed to a condition the regulator judged unacceptable.

The cost lands on everyone whose plans depend on a US departure. That includes US domestic travellers, but it also includes connecting passengers and freight forwarders in Asia, because a flight that cannot leave a US airport cannot arrive at a Malaysian one on schedule, and an aircraft stuck on the ground in the US cannot be turned around for the return leg.

The mechanism is a queue. A ground stop creates a backlog of aircraft waiting to depart. When the stop lifts, the backlog clears in sequence, and the delay propagates through the day's rotations. An aircraft that was due to fly into Kuala Lumpur arrives late, and the flight it was scheduled to operate back out departs late. The delay is not confined to the airport where the stop was issued.

The third actor is the regulator's credibility. A ground stop is a visible admission that normal operations cannot continue. The FAA has now used the instrument three times in twenty-five years, and the second and third uses both sit close to a technology event in the public record.
