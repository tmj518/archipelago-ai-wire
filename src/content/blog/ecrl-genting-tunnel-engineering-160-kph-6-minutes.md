## What happened

The East Coast Rail Link's Genting Tunnel is 16.39 kilometres long as built. It is a twin-bore tunnel, meaning two parallel tubes, and it carries the line through the Titiwangsa Range, the mountain spine that separates Peninsular Malaysia's west coast from its east coast.

ECRL Head of Infrastructure Design Mohd Rahiman Ab Rahman told Malay Mail that a train takes approximately 6.2 minutes to traverse the entire tunnel at an average cruising speed of 160 kilometres per hour. He also said passengers travel beneath Genting Highlands with as much as 750 metres of rock and earth separating them from the surface above.

The tunnel's safety design places emergency exits at 450-metre intervals, so passengers and crew can evacuate on foot in an emergency. The same article reports that the design incorporates a Climate Change Factor, with flood protection measures based on a 200-year return period — a standard meaning the design is sized for a flood of a severity expected once in 200 years.

The tunnel was bored by a machine built by China Communications Construction Group in Shanghai. That machine is 266 metres long, has a boring diameter of 8.98 metres, and weighs approximately 1,600 tonnes.

Every figure in this article comes from the Malay Mail article on the ECRL Genting Tunnel, published and retrieved on 19 November 2026. The article quotes Mohd Rahiman directly for the speed and crossing time; the remaining dimensions are reported in the same piece.

> **Twin-bore tunnel:** two separate parallel tubes, each carrying one track, rather than one wide tube carrying both. The arrangement separates the two directions of travel and gives each tube its own ventilation, its own emergency route and its own wall of rock between it and the other.

## So what

The gain lands on the passenger and the shipper who use the line. A tunnel removes the mountain as an obstacle. Before it, crossing the Titiwangsa Range meant a road journey over or around the range; with it, the crossing is a fixed 6.2 minutes at line speed, unaffected by the weather on the surface or the traffic on the road. That is the whole point of a tunnel: it converts a variable journey into a fixed one.

The cost lands on whoever maintains it. A tunnel with 750 metres of rock above it and exits every 450 metres is an asset with a permanent operating obligation: ventilation, drainage, lighting, inspection and emergency readiness, every day the line runs. That cost does not appear in a construction headline. It appears in the operator's budget for as long as the tunnel exists.

The third actor is the freight customer. The ECRL is a rail line, not a passenger service alone, and a tunnel that shortens the crossing shortens it for cargo as well. A container moving between the east coast and Port Klang gains the same fixed crossing a passenger does. The mechanism is the same in both cases: distance stops being measured in terrain and starts being measured in minutes.

Average cruising speed:  160 kilometres per hour

Time = distance / speed
     = 16.39 km / 160 km per hour
     = 0.1024375 hours
