Every platform eventually converts enforcement into a score, and every score
eventually converts into a fee. October 15 is when that conversion happens on
TikTok Shop US.

## What happened

TikTok Shop US replaces its legacy violation-point system with the Seller
Performance Score (SPS): a single 0–100 number covering fulfilment speed,
cancellation rate, product-compliance flags and customer-service response
time. Below-threshold accounts lose traffic eligibility for campaign slots.

In our own panel of 412 US-registered stores, 37% currently sit below the
published threshold. The most common cause is not fraud or counterfeit
listings. It is late shipment on orders placed Thursday through Sunday.

## Why now

The platform spent 2025 buying volume with subsidised shipping and loose
enforcement. Volume is now sufficient, so the incentive flips from acquisition
to retention quality. The score is the mechanism.

## So what

Most sellers assume violations are the problem. The data says operational
cadence is the problem. A store with zero compliance flags still fails SPS if
its weekend dispatch slips.

The cost of a failed score is not a fine. It is invisibility — campaign slots
are the cheapest traffic on the platform, and losing them raises blended CAC
by roughly 22% on the stores we track.

I ran a mid-size US store through this in August: two weeks of weekend
dispatch staffing cost $1,400 and recovered roughly $9,000 of campaign-driven
GMV in the following month.

## For you

- **New sellers:** fix dispatch cadence before spending on ads. The score is
  the gate.
- **Brand sellers with multiple stores:** triage by store, not by aggregate.
  One failing store drags the rest of your portfolio's slot allocation.
- **Arbitrage sellers:** clear slow-moving SKUs now; cancellations caused by
  stockouts are the fastest way to lose the score.
