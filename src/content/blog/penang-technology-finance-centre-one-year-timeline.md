## What happened

Penang Chief Minister Chow Kon Yeow told the state legislative assembly on 30 October 2026 that the state hopes to establish the proposed Penang Technology Finance Centre (PTFC) within one year. The original timeline was two years, which Chow acknowledged in his statement: "Initially, we said we wanted to see this realised within two years."

The PTFC is a designated financial technology centre for Malaysia. Chow said he was surprised when Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim, in his capacity as finance minister, announced in the 2027 Budget that Penang had been designated as the country's financial technology centre. The designation came from the federal government, and the state is now working to implement it.

The state also targets finalising the governance arrangements for a RM100 million strategic fund involving Khazanah and InvestPenang by year end. Khazanah is Malaysia's sovereign wealth fund, and InvestPenang is the state's investment promotion agency. The fund is a separate piece of the PTFC plan, with its own governance structure and timeline.

> **Penang Technology Finance Centre (PTFC):** a proposed centre designated in the 2027 Budget to serve as Malaysia's financial technology hub. The centre is intended to connect technology companies, especially in Penang's semiconductor and electrical and electronics (E&E) sectors, with financing and financial services tailored to their needs.

Penang has long been Malaysia's semiconductor and E&E manufacturing hub, and the state has been trying to capture more of the design, testing and financing activity that surrounds those factories. The PTFC is the latest step in that direction.

The compressed timeline is the notable change. The state originally planned two years to establish the centre.
