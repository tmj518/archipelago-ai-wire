## What happened

The Klang River was once one of the world's dirtiest waterways, designated a Class 5 severely polluted river under Malaysia's rankings. Years of work improved it to Class 3, or moderately polluted. That is the classification the river carries now, as reported by Malay Mail on 24 November 2026.

Malaysian wildlife ecologist Woo Chee Yoong told the publication that the improvement appears to have encouraged otters to move in. The animals are accused of raiding at least 12 houses in the past year. Resident Yee Sze Mun, 89, said otters killed an arowana fish he had nurtured for 15 years. Tropicana's security team believes there are around 25 otters in the forest and the estate, and the estate brought in Woo to teach residents how to deal with them.

> **River classification:** Malaysia grades river water by how clean it is, from Class 1 (cleanest, suitable for water supply with minimal treatment) down to Class 5 (severely polluted). ## Why now

The thread runs back to 2019, when local media began reporting otter sightings in Kuala Lumpur. Woo read those reports, and in the following year co-founded the Malaysian Otter Network. That is a seven-year record of a species appearing in a city river, tracked by a small conservation group rather than a government monitoring programme.

The second thread is the water. A river does not move from Class 5 to Class 3 in a season. It takes years of work on sewage, industrial discharge and rubbish entering the water. The classification is the measurable output of that work, and the otters are the visible one. Woo's statement to Malay Mail links the two: the cleaner water appears to have encouraged the animals to move in.

The third thread is the conflict. An otter is a fish eater. A river with fish in it supports otters; a suburb beside that river with ornamental fish ponds supports them better. The 12 raided houses and the lost arowana are the point at which a conservation success becomes a household cost, and that is why the story surfaced this month rather than in 2019.
