## What happened

The new EU FDI screening regulation is the instrument that moves the regime to 17 January 2028. No regulation number appears in the supplied source pack, so none is printed here. On 14 October 2026, the European Commission, Directorate-General for Trade and Economic Security, published the caseload figures that show what the incoming regime inherits.

EU Member States notified 667 investments to the Commission in 2025, up from 477 notifications in 2024. The Commission states that notifications to the EU cooperation mechanism increased by 40% between 2024 and 2025, the highest annual rise since the mechanism’s introduction.

The 2025 caseload split into two useful planning groups. Of the 667 cases notified, 88% closed within 15 days. Separately, 12% of notified investments required an in-depth security risk assessment, a 4% rise compared with 2024. The Commission issued opinions in less than 2% of cases, consistent with the pattern in 2024 and 2023.

## Why now

The timing matters because use of the system moved sharply before the regime change. The count increased by 190 notifications, rising from 477 in 2024 to 667 in 2025. The Commission describes that movement as the highest annual rise since the cooperation mechanism began.

The deeper-review share also moved. In 2025, 12% of notified investments required an in-depth security risk assessment, a 4% rise compared with 2024. At the same time, Commission opinions remained rare at less than 2% of cases, in keeping with 2024 and 2023.

The 17 January 2028 regime therefore arrives after a surge in notifications and a larger deep-review share, not after a quiet period. For an importer, the relevant response is to schedule around the observed closure pattern and separate ordinary closures from in-depth assessments.

## So what

The judgement is straightforward: these figures support timing triage, not a monetary landed-cost calculation. There is no duty rate, fee or penalty in the supplied statistics. The cost is the risk that financing, purchase-agreement completion, supplier integration, hiring, stock transfer or market launch shifts while screening continues.

For a transaction following the 88% pattern, the observed closure block is 15 days. If an agreement schedules completion 15 days after notification, the timetable matches the aggregate fast-closure group. If the commercial target is completion before 17 January 2028, the arithmetic reference point is 15 days before that target. This is reverse counting from an observed closure period, not a statement of a legal filing deadline.

The in-depth track requires different treatment. The 12% share equals 80.04 of the 667 notified cases, or 80 whole cases after rounding. For a single transaction, that figure is a frequency indicator, not a prediction. The source provides no common duration for in-depth assessments, so no fixed number of extra review days can be calculated from the supplied material.

The timing formula is:

`0.88 × 15 days + 0.12 × D`

The fast-track component equals 13.2 days. `D` represents the duration of an in-depth assessment, and the source does not supply `D`. A schedule containing only 15 days therefore reflects the observed ordinary closure group but does not quantify the complete timing risk for the 12% group.

The opinion statistic is narrower still. Less than 2% of 667 cases is below 13.34 cases, or 13 whole cases at the bound. The source does not state that opinions block transactions, impose charges or produce a fixed outcome. Treat the figure as evidence that Commission opinions were uncommon, not as a prohibition rate.

For a normalized portfolio of 100 notified investments, the aggregate split points to 88 cases in the 15-day closure group and 12 cases in the in-depth group. An importer with a multi-target pipeline can use that split to assign transaction-team capacity and decide which agreements need explicit review conditions. The aggregate figures do not identify differences by sector, investor origin, deal size or Member State, so they do not replace a transaction-specific notification analysis.

The practical planning rule is to build 15 days around the ordinary closure pattern and create a separate contingency for the in-depth track. Financing expiry, long-stop dates, purchase-price adjustments, integration launch plans and supplier communications should all distinguish between those two paths. ## FAQ

**Is the 15-day closure a legal deadline for every EU FDI notification?**

No. The supplied Commission figure says 88% of 667 notified cases closed within 15 days in 2025. It describes what happened across aggregate cases, not a universal procedural deadline. The remaining 12% required an in-depth security risk assessment, and the source material does not supply a common closure period for that track. Treat 15 days as the observed fast-track closure block for planning. Do not treat it as a guaranteed closing date, a fee trigger, or a substitute for the notification rules that apply to the specific investment.

**What monetary cost does the 17 January 2028 regime add to an import business?**

The supplied figures contain no duty rate, application fee, penalty amount or per-transaction charge. The cost shown here is timing risk around an EU acquisition, investment or corporate transaction. Notifications increased 40% from 477 in 2024 to 667 in 2025. That higher caseload increases the importance of planning around review outcomes. A transaction in the 88% group has an observed 15-day closure reference. A transaction in the 12% group enters an in-depth assessment with no aggregate duration in the source. Finance, integration and launch dates need to reflect that difference.

**How much timing contingency should an importer build before the regime starts?**

Use two blocks, not one. Put 15 days into the transaction schedule for the observed closure period associated with 88% of 2025 cases. Then add a separate in-depth-assessment contingency because 12% of notified investments entered that track. The source does not provide the duration of that track, so no fixed number of extra days is calculated here. The formula is 0.88 × 15 plus 0.12 × D, with D representing the unknown in-depth duration. This yields 13.2 days from the fast-track component alone, not a complete expected closure period. Confirm the applicable notification route and agreement conditions before setting a completion date.
