## What happened

Selangor Menteri Besar Datuk Seri Amirudin Shari said the number of flood hotspots in the state has dropped by 82.9 per cent, from 328 areas in the 2016/2017 period to 56 locations in 2026. Malay Mail reported the statement on 15 November 2026.

The same report says all remaining high-risk locations are being given special attention through an allocation for 47 mitigation projects aimed at reducing the impact of floods.

On the same day, the Social Welfare Department's (JKM) InfoBencana portal recorded 1,399 evacuees from 370 families still sheltering at six temporary relief centres in Kuala Selangor as of the afternoon. The Sekolah Kebangsaan (SK) Parit Mahang relief centre held the highest number of evacuees, at 420.

> **Flood hotspot:** a location the state identifies as prone to repeated flooding, and therefore a place where drainage, retention ponds or river works are prioritised. A hotspot count is a planning list, not a live flood map. A place can leave the list because the water was managed, or because the list was redrawn.

The two numbers describe different things and both are true at once. The hotspot count is a stock of known problem locations measured against a 2016/2017 baseline. The relief centre count is a flow of people displaced on one afternoon in November 2026.

## Why now

The statement lands at the start of the monsoon transition, the period when the northeast monsoon begins and rainfall patterns shift across the peninsula. This is the window in which state governments typically report on preparedness, because the question from residents changes from "what was built" to "what happens if it rains for three days".

The ten-year comparison is the thread. The 2016/2017 baseline is the period against which the current count is measured, which means the 82.9 per cent figure describes a decade of drainage, retention and river work rather than a single budget cycle. The 47 mitigation projects are the forward-looking half of the same story: the state is saying the remaining 56 locations are known, named and funded.

The relief centre numbers explain why the announcement surfaced this month rather than earlier. Kuala Selangor is a coastal district at the mouth of the Selangor River, and the evacuee count shows that the monsoon transition is already producing displacement while the state reports progress on its hotspot list. Both facts belong to the same week.

## So what

The gain lands on residents of the locations that left the list. A household that flooded repeatedly in the 2016/2017 period and has not flooded since has received something real: drainage that works, a retention pond that holds, a river that was widened. That is the mechanism behind the 82.9 per cent, and it is worth naming plainly because it is the part of the story that improves lives.

The cost lands on the 56 locations still on the list, and on the households inside them. A place stays on a hotspot list because water still arrives there. The 47 mitigation projects are the state's answer, but a project under allocation is not a project completed, and the report does not say how many of the 47 are finished, underway or not yet started.

The third group is the one the hotspot count cannot see: the 370 families in Kuala Selangor relief centres on 15 November 2026. A hotspot list is built from historical flooding. A family can be displaced by water that arrived through a route the list does not track, or by rainfall heavy enough to overwhelm a system that performs well in an ordinary year. The count and the displacement measure different things, and the state's progress on the first does not answer for the second.

## What it means for Malaysia

The consequence lands on Selangor households, and on the local councils, drainage departments and district offices that carry the monsoon season every year.

The concrete carrier is the household flood plan. A family in one of the 56 remaining hotspot locations has a different decision from a family in a location that left the list. The first family should treat the coming weeks as an active risk period: know the nearest relief centre, keep documents and medication in one bag, and know which route out of the neighbourhood stays open when water rises. The second family has evidence that its drainage improved, which is a reason to check the assumption rather than to drop it.

The second carrier is the small business. A shop or workshop in a low-lying area carries stock, equipment and a lease. Flood cover and stock placement are decisions made before the rain, not during it. The hotspot list is the state's own statement about which areas carry the highest risk, and a business inside one of the 56 locations is being told something specific by its own state government.

The third carrier is the relief centre itself. SK Parit Mahang held 420 evacuees, the largest single centre in the Kuala Selangor group. Schools that double as relief centres carry a double function in the monsoon weeks, and parents in those districts face the practical question of whether the school day survives the water.

## How we calculated this

The 82.9 per cent figure is the state's own. The arithmetic below reproduces it from the two counts the statement gives, so a reader can see what the percentage is measuring.

```text
Hotspots in 2016/2017:              328
Hotspots in 2026:                    56
Reduction in locations:             328 - 56 = 272
Reduction as share of baseline:     272 / 328 = 0.8293
Expressed as a percentage:          0.8293 x 100 = 82.9 per cent

Average evacuees per family:        1,399 / 370 = 3.78 people per family
Share of evacuees at SK Parit Mahang: 420 / 1,399 = 0.3002 = 30.0 per cent
```

The first block confirms the state's headline. The second block describes the Kuala Selangor group: roughly four people per displaced family, and about three in ten evacuees sheltering at a single school.

## FAQ

**Does the 82.9 per cent reduction mean Selangor no longer floods?**
No. The figure compares two hotspot lists ten years apart: 328 areas in 2016/2017 against 56 locations in 2026, as stated by Selangor Menteri Besar Datuk Seri Amirudin Shari and reported by Malay Mail on 15 November 2026. A hotspot list is a planning document built from past flooding, and it records where the state has prioritised drainage and river works. It does not record every place water reaches in a heavy season. On the same day the reduction was reported, the Social Welfare Department's InfoBencana portal recorded 1,399 evacuees from 370 families in six relief centres in Kuala Selangor. Both facts are true, and they answer different questions.

**What are the 47 mitigation projects and do they cover my area?**
The report says all remaining high-risk locations are being given special attention through an allocation for 47 mitigation projects aimed at reducing the impact of floods. It does not list the projects, their locations, their cost or their completion status. A resident who wants to know whether their street is covered needs the state's project list, which this report does not publish. What the report does establish is that the state has identified 56 remaining hotspot locations and attached a programme of works to them. Whether a specific address sits inside one of those 56 is a question for the local council or district office.

**What should a household in a flood-prone part of Selangor do before the monsoon peaks?**
Treat the state's hotspot list as a warning about your own address. If you are in one of the 56 remaining locations, the practical steps are the ones that work before water arrives: keep identity documents, medication and a phone charger in one bag, confirm the nearest relief centre and the route to it, and check whether your home insurance covers flood damage. If your area left the list since 2016/2017, the improvement is real evidence but not a guarantee, so check whether your street flooded in the last two seasons. The 420 evacuees at SK Parit Mahang on 15 November 2026 show how quickly a school becomes a shelter.

## The point

A ten-year drop from 328 flood hotspots to 56 is a genuine piece of public work, and the households that stopped flooding are the proof of it. The number that matters this week is smaller and harder: 370 families in Kuala Selangor relief centres on 15 November 2026. A state can shrink its hotspot list and still be carrying displaced households in the same afternoon, and a resident's flood plan should be built on the second number as much as the first.
