> **Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC):** the committee inside the US central bank that meets on set dates to decide the federal funds rate, the benchmark rate for US dollar borrowing. Its decisions are announced on the day the meeting ends, and financial markets worldwide reprice dollar-linked assets within minutes of the announcement.

The press release describes the schedule as tentative and revised. That wording matters: the dates are a planning calendar, not a commitment that a rate change happens on any of them. A meeting can end with the rate unchanged.

## Why now

Central banks publish their meeting calendars well ahead of the year they cover, so the 2027 schedule appearing in November 2026 follows a routine administrative cycle rather than a policy event. The Federal Reserve Board's own description of the document as a revised tentative schedule confirms it is a planning notice.

The reason it is worth a Malaysian reader's attention is the two-to-three-year thread it sits inside. Since the US began raising rates sharply to fight inflation, dollar borrowing has become more expensive for everyone outside the United States, and emerging-market currencies including the ringgit have felt the pull of a stronger dollar. When the US rate is high, money tends to move toward dollar assets, and currencies like the ringgit face downward pressure. When the US rate falls, some of that pressure eases.

Malaysia's own interest-rate setting runs on a separate calendar. Bank Negara Malaysia, the central bank, sets the Overnight Policy Rate, the benchmark for ringgit lending, at its own meetings. The two calendars are independent, but they are connected: a widening gap between US and Malaysian rates pulls money across borders and shows up in the exchange rate. Readers who follow the ringgit already track the Malaysian decisions; the 2027 schedule adds the US side of the same comparison. ## FAQ

**Does the 2027 schedule tell me whether US rates will rise or fall?**
No. The Federal Reserve Board press release of 12 November 2026 lists meeting dates only. It states no rate, no target and no direction. A meeting can end with the US policy rate unchanged, raised or lowered, and the schedule does not indicate which. Anyone presenting the calendar as a forecast is adding something the document does not contain. What the schedule does give a Malaysian reader is the timing: the six dates on which the US side of the dollar comparison is reset, so that a household or firm with dollar exposure knows when to expect news rather than being surprised by it.

**How does a US rate decision reach a Malaysian household?**
Through the dollar. The FOMC sets the US policy rate, which moves the value of the dollar against other currencies, including the ringgit. A stronger dollar raises the ringgit price of imported goods, overseas tuition and dollar-denominated loan repayments. A weaker dollar does the opposite. The path is indirect and takes time: the decision is announced, dollar assets reprice, the exchange rate moves, and the change reaches prices and repayments afterwards. The schedule marks the points at which the first step happens. It does not determine the size or direction of what follows.

**Is Malaysia's own interest rate set on the same dates?**
No. Bank Negara Malaysia sets the Overnight Policy Rate at its own meetings on its own calendar, separate from the Federal Reserve's. The two are connected through the exchange rate and cross-border money flows, but neither institution sets its rate on the other's schedule. A Malaysian borrower with a ringgit loan is directly affected by the OPR, and indirectly by US rates through the exchange rate and through the funding costs of the banks that lend to them. The 2027 FOMC dates are the US half of that picture, not the domestic half.
