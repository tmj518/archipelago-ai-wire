The measure takes the form of tariff-rate quotas coupled with price thresholds. The supplied source identifies the Directorate-General for Trade and Economic Security as the issuing body, but it does not provide a separate regulation number.

The safeguard investigation opened in March 2026. After reviewing input from interested parties, the Commission concluded that provisional measures were urgently needed. A safeguard investigation normally completes within nine months; in exceptional circumstances, the period extends to 11 months. Any definitive Commission proposal requires support from at least 15 EU Member States representing 65% of the EU population in the Safeguard Committee.

> **Tariff-rate quota (TRQ)** — a trade measure that applies a specified set of terms to imports entering within a quota volume and a different set after that volume is exhausted. The Commission couples the GOES TRQ with a price threshold, so quota status and invoice price both matter.

## So what

The announcement changes contract risk immediately, but it does not supply enough numerical detail to produce a final landed-cost amount. A GOES importer must verify the operative product code, the quota balance for that code, the applicable price threshold, and the charge that applies outside the quota or below the threshold.

Use these symbols when checking a shipment:

- `q` = declared quantity
- `v` = customs value
- `u` = unit price, calculated as `v / q`
- `P` = operative price threshold
- `R` = operative out-of-quota charge rate
- `Q` = remaining quota volume

| --- | --- | --- |
| Quota open; unit price at or above `P` | Apply in-quota terms after verifying requirements | No quota volume or threshold value supplied |
| Quota open; unit price below `P` | Threshold gap = `(P − u) × q` | `P` not supplied |
| Quota exhausted | Out-of-quota charge = `R × v` | `R` not supplied |
| Laminations, cores, or transformers in scope | Determine classification, then apply relevant row | Tariff codes not supplied |

For a direct GOES purchase, the threshold gap shows the price increase needed to bring a low-priced shipment to the threshold. If a supplier’s unit price sits below `P`, multiplying the difference by the declared quantity gives the total commercial uplift. The importer then compares that uplift with the official charge imposed for below-threshold entries.

For a shipment arriving after quota exhaustion, the exposure follows the customs value and the operative out-of-quota charge. Multiplying `R` by `v` gives the charge, but the supplied announcement contains no value for `R`. Using an assumed steel duty would produce a false landed-cost number.

Downstream goods require the same discipline. Cut laminations, assembled cores, and finished transformers containing GOES cores do not automatically share the classification of the raw steel. The scope answer determines whether the TRQ and threshold formulas apply at all. Importers of goods containing steel can use the material-composition approach discussed in [the CBAM downstream extension analysis](/blog/eu-cbam-downstream-extension-material-composition-2028/) and compare safeguard treatment with other border charges in [the overlapping EU steel-charge calculation](/blog/eu-cbam-vs-steel-safeguard-duty-per-tonne-2026/).

## FAQ

**Are steel laminations, cores, and transformers containing a GOES core covered by the provisional safeguard?**

The supplied Commission announcement identifies “grain-oriented electrical steel products” but does not provide tariff codes or a product list. That omission leaves the downstream boundary unresolved. Cut laminations are processed GOES, assembled cores are further manufactured, and a transformer is a finished machine. Each form can have a different tariff classification and a different answer under the operative product scope. Treat coil or sheet, laminations, cores, and transformers containing cores as separate classification tasks. Obtain the operative regulation text or a binding customs ruling for the exact code before purchase.

**What quota volume and price threshold apply to each GOES product?**

The source confirms tariff-rate quotas coupled with price thresholds but supplies no quota volume, quota allocation, threshold price, currency, unit of quantity, or out-of-quota charge. No reliable landed-cost figure follows from the announcement alone. Importers need the operative annex or customs notice for the exact product code, the remaining quota balance at declaration, the threshold basis, and the charge that applies when price falls below it or quota is exhausted. Do not substitute an estimated threshold or a generic steel duty in a purchase contract. Verify each term in writing.

**When does the investigation finish and do provisional measures become definitive?**

The investigation opened in March 2026 and normally completes within nine months. In exceptional circumstances, the completion period extends to 11 months. The provisional measures apply from 18 September 2026 while that process continues. A definitive outcome is not automatic. The Commission proposal requires support from at least 15 EU Member States representing 65% of the EU population in the Safeguard Committee. Importers should track both the investigation clock and the committee vote, because a definitive measure can alter duration and terms after provisional cargo has cleared.
