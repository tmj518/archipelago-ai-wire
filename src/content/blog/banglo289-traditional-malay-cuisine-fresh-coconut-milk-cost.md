## So what

The gain lands on the customer who wants Malay food cooked the way it is cooked at home, and on the chain that can serve it at a mid-market price. RM30 to RM50 a head is not fine dining and not a hawker stall. It is the band where a household eats out on an occasion, and where the kitchen has to be consistent across four locations.

The cost lands on the operator's working capital. Fresh coconut milk is bought, delivered, cooked and sold within a short window. Every kilogram that is not sold is money lost twice: once at purchase and once at disposal. The batch size of 10 to 15 servings is the mechanism that limits that exposure. A kitchen that cooks 60 servings of a coconut-milk dish at once and sells 40 has wasted a third of a perishable input. A kitchen that cooks 15, sells out, and cooks again has taken a second labour cost instead of a waste cost. The chain has chosen the second trade.

The third actor is the supplier. A single buyer taking RM50,000 of fresh coconut milk a month across four outlets is a meaningful customer for a coconut mill or a distributor. That gives the buyer negotiating room on price and on delivery frequency, and it gives the supplier a reason to prioritise the account. Neither side has published the terms.
