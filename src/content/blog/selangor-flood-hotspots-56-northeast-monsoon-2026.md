## What happened

The Selangor Fire and Rescue Department (JBPM) has identified 56 flood hotspots and 232 flood-prone areas across the state ahead of the Northeast Monsoon 2026. The department's figures were reported by Malay Mail on 16 November 2026.

The two lists are not the same list. A hotspot is a place the department expects water to gather first. A flood-prone area is a wider zone where water has reached before. The department counts 56 of the first and 232 of the second.

The district breakdown is uneven. Klang carries the most hotspots, with 13, followed by Petaling (11), Gombak (10), Hulu Langat (nine), Kuala Selangor (six), Sepang (three), and Kuala Langat and Hulu Selangor with two each. On the flood-prone list the order changes: Hulu Langat has the most at 37, followed by Klang (33), Sepang (32), Kuala Selangor and Gombak (29 each), Petaling (27), Kuala Langat (22), Hulu Selangor (12) and Sabak Bernam (11).

> **Flood hotspot:** a specific location the Fire and Rescue Department expects to flood first when heavy rain arrives, and where it positions people and boats before the water rises. A **flood-prone area** is a broader zone with a history of flooding. A district can rank high on one list and lower on the other, because the two measure different things.

The department has put 1,603 firefighters and 1,251 volunteer members on standby, supported by 71 rescue boats, 12 operational lorries and 68 four-wheel-drive vehicles. It also named a rainfall threshold: more than 100 millimetres of rain within just over two hours can trigger flash floods in Kampung Melayu Subang and surrounding areas if rivers and local drainage cannot cope with the volume.

## Why now

The Northeast Monsoon is the annual window when the wind shifts and heavy rain falls across the peninsula's east coast and, in bad years, across Selangor as well. The department publishes its hotspot and flood-prone lists before that window opens, so that equipment and people are placed before the first flood rather than after it.

The second thread is recent. The report ties the timing to a specific incident: following flooding, Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim on 3 November 2026 directed that implementation of the nearly RM700 million Sungai Buloh and Sungai Damansara Flood Mitigation Projects be expedited. Two rivers, two projects, one instruction to move faster.

The third thread is the gap between building and flooding. Flood mitigation projects take years to design, fund and construct. The monsoon arrives every year on schedule. The department's list is what fills that gap in the meantime: it is the operational answer for the seasons before the concrete is finished.

This is not a new problem in Selangor. The state has published hotspot counts before, and the long-run trend has been downward. Our earlier report on [Selangor flood hotspots falling 82.9 per cent over ten years](/blog/selangor-flood-hotspots-drop-829-percent-2026/) sets out that longer record, and it is worth reading alongside this piece: fewer hotspots than a decade ago, but 1,399 people still in relief centres at the time of that report.

## So what

The gain lands on residents of the named districts who act on the list. A household in Klang, Petaling, Gombak or Hulu Langat now knows, before the rain, that its district sits near the top of one of the two lists. That knowledge is only worth something if it changes what the household does this week: documents in a plastic folder, a bag packed, a plan for where the car goes and where the family meets.

The cost lands on the same households if the warning does not translate into action. A list of 56 hotspots and 232 flood-prone areas is a map, not a barrier. The department's 1,603 firefighters and 1,251 volunteers are a response force, not a prevention force. They arrive after the water. The household that has not prepared absorbs the first hours of a flood alone.

The third actor is the district office and the local council. A district with 37 flood-prone areas, as Hulu Langat has, carries a heavier load of drainage clearing, relief centre preparation and road closure planning than a district with 11. The list tells those offices where to spend the weeks before the monsoon, and it tells residents which offices to call.

## What it means for Malaysia

The consequence lands on Selangor households, and through them on the state's employers. Selangor is Malaysia's most industrialised state, and Klang in particular is a port and manufacturing district. A flood in Klang does not stay in Klang: it reaches the warehouse, the shift roster and the delivery schedule of firms whose workers live in the affected areas.

The concrete carrier is the commute and the shift. A worker in Klang whose road floods cannot reach the factory. A small business whose stock sits in a low-lying shoplot in Petaling loses it if the water arrives before the sandbags. A family in Hulu Langat whose house floods moves to a relief centre, and the wage earner stops earning for the days the water stays.

The second carrier is the mitigation project itself. The nearly RM700 million Sungai Buloh and Sungai Damansara projects, once built, change the flood map for the areas they drain. Until then, the department's list is the working map.
