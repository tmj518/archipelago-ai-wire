## What happened

The Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ) recorded RM88.83 billion in approved investments between January 2025 and June 2026. Economy Minister Akmal Nasir gave the figure, and FMT reported it on 2 November 2026. The same total carries the potential to create 28,755 jobs.

Approved is not the same as built. Of the RM88.83 billion, 45 per cent, or RM40.30 billion, had been realised as of June 2026. Those realised projects are expected to generate 12,796 jobs. The remaining share sits in approvals that have not yet turned into spending on buildings, machines and payroll.

Two dates sit alongside the money. Both countries agreed to organise a joint investment forum in the first quarter of 2027. A JS-SEZ blueprint is scheduled to be launched by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim and Singapore prime minister Lawrence Wong in December 2026.

> **Approved investment:** a project that a government investment authority has cleared to proceed, usually with tax incentives attached. It is a permission and an intention, not money already spent. A realised investment is the part that has actually been committed and is moving through construction, equipment and hiring.

## Why now

The JS-SEZ is a joint Malaysia-Singapore project to pull investment into Johor by pairing Malaysian land and labour with Singaporean capital and connections. The thread runs back to the agreement that set the zone up, and the numbers now arriving are the first substantial read on whether it is working.

What surfaced this month is the realisation rate. A headline approvals figure tells a reader how much interest the zone has attracted. The 45 per cent share tells a reader how much of that interest has turned into projects on the ground. Publishing both, in the same statement, is the substantive change: it gives businesses and workers a way to judge the zone on delivery rather than on announcements.

The second reason it surfaced now is the calendar. A blueprint is due in December 2026, and a joint investment forum in the first quarter of 2027. Both are forward-looking events, and a minister setting out the numbers ahead of them is establishing the baseline against which those events will be judged.

## So what

The gain lands on the firms that build and supply inside the zone, and on the workers those projects hire. A realised investment is money that reaches a construction site, an equipment order and a payroll. The 12,796 jobs attached to realised projects are the part of the promise that has a project behind it.

The cost lands on the gap. Roughly half the approved money has not been realised, and an approval that never becomes a project ties up land, incentives and administrative attention without producing a job. The mechanism is a queue: approvals enter, projects move through permitting, site preparation and financing, and only some reach the point where they spend. The realisation rate is the measure of how fast that queue clears.

The third actor is the Singapore side. A joint forum in the first quarter of 2027 is a move to convert interest into commitments, and it tells a reader that both governments see the pipeline as something to be worked rather than something that fills itself. That is a scenario about intent, not a forecast of amounts.

## How we calculated this

The arithmetic compares what has been realised against what has been approved, and what that leaves outstanding.

```text
Approved investments, January 2025 to June 2026:  RM88.83 billion
Realised investments, as of June 2026:            RM40.30 billion
Share realised, as stated:                        45%

Check: RM40.30 billion / RM88.83 billion = 0.4537, i.e. about 45%

Outstanding (approved but not yet realised):
RM88.83 billion - RM40.30 billion = RM48.53 billion

Jobs:
Potential jobs from all approved investments:     28,755
Jobs from realised projects:                      12,796
Jobs attached to the unrealised balance:
28,755 - 12,796 = 15,959
```

The RM48.53 billion and the 15,959 jobs are derived here from the minister's figures; the source does not state them directly.
