## What happened

The East Coast Rail Link's first phase will be launched on 12 December 2026. Deputy Transport Minister Hasbi Habibollah told the Dewan Rakyat that the launch will be officiated by Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, as reported by Free Malaysia Today on 19 November 2026.

Phase 1 runs 523km from Kota Bharu in Kelantan to Gombak in Selangor. It carries 16 stations, a mix of passenger stations and combined passenger-and-cargo terminals.

The launch lands in a gap. Loke Siew Fook resigned as transport minister on 10 November 2026, handing in his letter of resignation in protest over the house-arrest issue involving former prime minister Najib Razak. The portfolio is now empty.

That vacancy produced the dispute. Wee Ka Siong, the Ayer Hitam MP, asked the House how the ECRL service can be launched without the formal approval of a transport minister under the Land Public Transport Act 2010. Loke replied that the missing signature is a trivial matter, and said the power to act is set out in Article 39 of the Federal Constitution.

> **Land Public Transport Act 2010:** the statute that governs rail and road public transport services in Peninsular Malaysia. It sets out who can operate a service and under what approval. The dispute here is narrow: whether a rail service can begin carrying passengers without a sitting minister's formal sign-off, or whether another office can supply that approval while the portfolio is vacant.

The two phases of the line, and the dates attached to each, are the facts a reader needs before judging the argument.

## Why now

Three threads converge in the same fortnight.

The first is the construction calendar. Phase 1 is finished enough to be launched on a fixed date, and the operator has a second phase still under construction with a completion target of December 2027 and operations scheduled from January 2028. A launch date is set months ahead because it anchors testing, staffing and ticketing. Moving it is expensive; holding it is cheap.

The second is the ministerial vacancy. Loke resigned on 10 November 2026. The launch is 12 December 2026. That leaves a window in which the portfolio is empty and a statutory approval is, on Wee's reading, outstanding. The question surfaced in the Dewan Rakyat this month because the gap between the resignation and the launch is now short enough to see.

The third is the political context Loke named in his resignation: the house-arrest issue involving Najib Razak. That is the reason the portfolio emptied, and it is the reason the dispute is being argued in public rather than settled inside the ministry. A resignation on a matter of principle turns an administrative step into a political one.

## So what

The gain lands on the operator and on the east-coast economies the line was built to serve. A launched line begins the slow work of building ridership, signing cargo customers and proving the timetable. Kelantan and the Gombak end of the Klang Valley get a rail connection that did not exist before.

The cost lands on legal certainty. A service that starts while its approval is contested carries a question mark, even if the question is later answered. The mechanism is procedural: if a court or the House later finds the approval was required and absent, the remedy is not to un-build the railway but to regularise the paperwork after the fact. That is untidy, and it is the kind of untidiness that invites a challenge to the next approval in the chain.

The third actor is the opposition. Wee's question is a procedural one with a political payload: it puts on the record that a major national project is launching without a minister in place. The response from Loke, that the matter is trivial, is itself a political move. Both are arguing about a signature, not about whether the trains run.
