## AI Summary

China's real-name registration rules took effect on 1 May 2026, adding a standalone confirmation form to company registration, on top of an agent registry live since September 2025 that refuses filings for three years from anyone designated responsible for a false registration. Both give a buyer a way to check who actually filed a supplier's registration. And the arithmetic that decides whether to spend $150 on any check at all: it breaks even at a 0.83 per cent chance of a bad supplier.

## What happened

Almost every first order from a new buyer goes wrong in the same place: the deposit is paid to a company that does not own the machines. The signal has always been checkable, and in the last thirteen months China has added two registers that make it cheaper to check.

| Check | Factory | Trading company |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business scope on the licence | Manufacturing, processing | Trading, import and export |
| Address | Industrial park, own workshop | Office building, or a room number |
| Product range | Narrow and deep | Everything under one roof |
| Quotation style | Per process, tooling quoted separately | Instant, all-inclusive |
| Video call | Walks the floor | Shows a showroom, or the line is busy |

**What is new is the paper trail behind the licence.** The 2026 editions of the company registration document and materials standards took effect on **1 May 2026**, replacing the 2022 editions, and the substantive addition is a standalone real-name registration confirmation form. Separately, the national registration-agent information system went live on **25 September 2025** under a rule effective 15 September 2025. That rule refuses filings for **three years** from a person designated directly responsible for a false registration, counted from the date the registration is revoked, and requires agents to retain business and transaction records for at least **ten years**.

> **Registration agent** — the intermediary who files a company's registration with the market regulator. Every registered Chinese company has one, and the agent registry makes the agent's identity and history part of the public record. This matters to a buyer for one reason: a manufacturer files its own registration and a trading company's registration is frequently filed by an agent who files for many companies. Asking which agent filed it, and getting a consistent answer, is a test rather than a formality.

## Why now

Two rules landed inside thirteen months, and they point the same way: registration is being tied to identifiable people rather than to a name on a form.

The agent system has been live since September 2025 with its three-year filing ban and ten-year retention. The real-name confirmation form arrived with the 2026 standards on 1 May 2026. Neither was written for foreign buyers. Both happen to give a buyer something that did not exist before: **a way to ask who submitted a registration and to check whether that answer holds.**

There is a commercial reason this matters now as well. Purchasing prices for Chinese industrial producers rose **5.8 per cent** year on year in August 2026, and **3.2 per cent** on average across January to August, while producer prices for consumer goods fell **0.5 per cent**. **The gap between what a factory pays for inputs and what it receives for output is the space a trading company lives in** — and in a rising-input year, a trader's margin is under pressure, which is when the pricing differences between a factory and an intermediary tend to widen rather than close.

## So what

The verification order below costs nothing until the last step, and the last step is the only one that costs real money. Whether to run it is an arithmetic question, not a judgement call.

**The break-even, worked.** Suppose you spend $150 on a check, and suppose a bad supplier costs you the deposit:

| Order value | Deposit at 20 per cent | Loss avoided | Break-even risk on a $150 check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $25,000 | $5,000 | $5,000 | 3.0 per cent |
| $50,000 | $10,000 | $10,000 | 1.5 per cent |
| $90,000 | $18,000 | $18,000 | 0.83 per cent |

**At the largest of those, a 0.83 per cent chance of a bad supplier is enough.** That is one supplier in 120. Where tooling and moulds are paid up front as well as the deposit, the loss avoided rises and the threshold falls further. Buyers who run the free steps first find the rate well above that — which is why the deposit-risk model on this site puts the threshold below one per cent for larger orders and treats the check as obviously worth running rather than marginal.

**And the free steps catch most of the field.** Steps 1 to 3 cost nothing but an hour, and they are the ones that turn "who am I talking to" into a fact.

## For you

- **First-time importer:** run steps 1 to 3 on every supplier you shortlist. They cost an hour and eliminate most of the field. Do them before any deposit conversation, not as part of it.
- **Buyer with an existing supplier:** re-run step 2 once a year. Ownership, scope and addresses change, and the agent behind a registration can change too, which is the new part.
- **Anyone who has been quoted "factory prices" without a factory:** ask which agent filed the registration. A manufacturer usually knows; an intermediary usually has to check, and the pause is the answer.
- **Buyer budgeting for a first order:** treat the $150 inspection as a line item rather than a decision. At a 0.83 per cent break-even it is cheaper than the conversation about who owns the deposit.
- **Anyone negotiating a 2027 price now:** the input side is up 3.2 per cent year to date and the output side is flat to down, so a supplier quoting an unchanged price is absorbing a real gap somewhere. That gap is worth asking about directly — see [the input-cost gap and what it means for a quote](/blog/china-input-cost-gap-supplier-price-negotiation-2027/).

## FAQ

### What is the cheapest way to tell a factory from a trading company?

Ask for the full Chinese legal name rather than the English trade name, then check the business scope on the registration. Manufacturing and processing means a factory; trading and import-export means an intermediary. English names are unregistered, so two different companies can trade under the same one, and the registered name is the only identifier that resolves to a single entity. The lookup is free, takes minutes, and it is the step buyers skip most often because it feels like paperwork rather than verification. It is the cheapest thing in the whole process and it eliminates most of a shortlist before any money is discussed.

### What changed on 1 May 2026?

The 2026 editions of the company registration document and materials standards took effect, replacing the 2022 editions. The substantive addition is a standalone real-name registration confirmation form. It does not change what a foreign buyer must do, and nothing about it is aimed at export trade. What it changes is what sits on file: registration is now tied to an identified person as well as to a company, which makes the public record more useful in a supplier check than it was a year ago. A buyer who asks which agent filed a registration is asking a question the register can now answer.

### How does the agent registry help a buyer?

The national registration-agent information system has been live since 25 September 2025. Ask a supplier which agent or agency filed their registration, then treat the answer as a test rather than a formality. The rule requires agents to retain records for at least ten years and refuses filings for three years from a person designated responsible for a false registration, so the agent's identity carries consequences that a name on a form does not. A manufacturer typically knows its own filing history. An intermediary typically has to go and check, and that pause is itself the answer.

### Is a $150 inspection worth it on a small order?

It depends on what a bad supplier costs you, and the division is short. On a $25,000 order with a 20 per cent deposit the loss is $5,000 and the check breaks even at a 3 per cent risk; on $50,000 it breaks even at 1.5 per cent; on $90,000 it falls to 0.83 per cent. Where tooling and moulds are paid up front as well as the deposit, the loss avoided is larger and the threshold falls further still. Put your own deposit figure into that division rather than judging the fee against the order value.

### Can I skip verification if the supplier has a certificate?

No, and this is the mistake that costs the most after the deposit. Certificates are issued per factory and per product, so a certificate is evidence about a specific production site rather than about the entity invoicing you. An intermediary can hold a genuine certificate that belongs to a factory you are not buying from, and the document will look correct. Ask for the certificate number and check it against the issuing body, not against the supplier's own website, which is a page the supplier controls and can change.

## How we calculated this

The break-even is the inspection cost divided by the loss avoided; below that probability, the check is worth running.

```text
break-even probability = cost of the check / loss avoided

  check                  $150
  order value            $50,000
  deposit (assumed)      20%
  loss avoided           = $50,000 x 20% = $10,000
  break-even             = $150 / $10,000 = 1.5%

  with tooling and moulds paid up front:
  loss avoided           = $18,000
  break-even             = $150 / $18,000 = 0.83%

input-cost context, August 2026, National Bureau of Statistics
  purchasing prices, industrial producers    +5.8% year on year
  producer prices, industrial products       +3.8% year on year
  purchasing prices, January to August       +3.2% average
  producer prices, consumer goods            -0.5% year on year
```

Put your own deposit percentage and order value into the first line. The model is a division, and the answer changes the moment your numbers do.

## The point

Supplier verification is not about finding out whether a company is honest. It is about finding out whether the entity you are paying is the entity that makes your product, because everything else in the order depends on that answer being yes.

China has spent the last thirteen months making that question easier to answer — a real-name confirmation form from 1 May 2026 and an agent registry with a three-year filing ban behind it. Neither was built for foreign buyers, and both are usable by them. The free steps take an hour, and the paid step breaks even at less than one supplier in 120.
