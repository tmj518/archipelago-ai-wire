## What happened

The Genting Tunnel on the East Coast Rail Link (ECRL) is 16.39 kilometres long, according to a Malay Mail article published on 19 November 2026. The report describes it as the longest rail tunnel in Southeast Asia. It is a twin-bore tunnel, meaning two parallel tubes, and it carries the railway beneath the Titiwangsa Range, the mountain spine that separates Peninsular Malaysia's east coast from the Klang Valley.

The engineering numbers in the report are specific. At the maximum point, 750 metres of rock and earth sit between the tunnel roof and the surface above. Emergency exits are placed at 450-metre intervals so passengers and crew can leave on foot in an emergency. The tunnel was bored by a machine built in Shanghai by China Communications Construction Group: 266 metres long, 8.98 metres in boring diameter, and weighing about 1,600 tonnes. At its fastest, that machine excavated 700 metres of tunnel in a month.

The travel figure comes from ECRL Head of Infrastructure Design Mohd Rahiman Ab Rahman, quoted in the same article. A train crossing the full tunnel at an average cruising speed of 160 kilometres per hour takes approximately 6.2 minutes.

> **Tunnel boring machine (TBM):** a large machine that cuts a circular tunnel through rock or soil and installs the concrete lining behind it as it advances. It is used instead of drilling and blasting where the ground must be disturbed as little as possible, and it is assembled on site because it is too large to ship whole.

The report also states that the tunnel's design incorporates a Climate Change Factor, with flood protection measures based on a 200-year return period — a standard meaning a flood of that size is expected once in 200 years on average.

That is the language of a structure that has been built, not one that is being proposed.

The thread runs back through the construction itself. A tunnel of 16.39 kilometres bored at a maximum of 700 metres per month is a job measured in years, not months, and the machine that did it had to be built in Shanghai and assembled at the portal. The report's detail — the 266-metre machine, the 8.98-metre bore, the 1,600 tonnes — is the kind of specification released when the work is far enough along to be described rather than defended.

The third thread is the mountain. The Titiwangsa Range is the reason the ECRL needs a tunnel at all: a railway running from the East Coast to the Klang Valley has to cross it, and crossing it at surface level means curves, gradients and land acquisition. A tunnel trades those problems for one large, fixed cost. The 750 metres of overburden is the measure of how completely the railway has been taken out of the landscape.

## How we calculated this

The only honest arithmetic in the source is the relationship between the tunnel's length, the quoted crossing time and the quoted cruising speed. The figures check against each other, which is a reason to trust the report's numbers as a set.

```text
Tunnel length:            16.39 kilometres
Cruising speed:           160 kilometres per hour
Time at that speed:       16.39 / 160 = 0.1024 hours
Convert to minutes:       0.1024 x 60 = 6.15 minutes
Reported crossing time:   6.2 minutes
Difference:               6.2 - 6.15 = 0.05 minutes (about 3 seconds)

Excavation check:
Tunnel length:            16.39 kilometres = 16,390 metres
Maximum rate:             700 metres per month
Months at maximum rate:   16,390 / 700 = 23.4 months
```

The crossing time in the report is consistent with the length and the speed to within a few seconds, which is what a rounded figure looks like. The excavation check gives the fastest possible boring schedule for one tube at the machine's maximum rate: about 23.4 months. The report does not state how long the boring actually took, so that figure is a floor, not a record.
