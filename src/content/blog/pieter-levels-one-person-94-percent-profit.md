<h1>Pieter Levels: A One-Person Business Posting $200K–$250K a Month in Revenue</h1>
<p>Pieter Levels runs Nomads.com (formerly Nomad List) and Remote OK as a solo operator with no employees and no funding, and in a 23 September 2026 post he quoted his business revenue as having "remained very stable at about $200K-$250K/mo" while stating his profit margin was about 94.5% and his total business expenses had fallen to roughly $300 per month, excluding Photo AI's GPU and storage bills. Those figures are self-reported on his own site. They are not audited. This article sticks to what his published posts, the Remote OK FAQ, and the live Nomads.com homepage actually say, and marks clearly where the public record stops.</p>

<h2>Who Pieter Levels Is</h2>
<p>The reviewed sources do not state his nationality, his date of birth, or where he is based today, and this article will not guess. What the sources do establish is thinner but more useful: he publishes under the handle @levelsio, writes long-running posts at levels.io, sells a book called MAKE, and describes a decade-plus track record of building products without outside money. In his February 2026 post he writes that he has been doing this "for 10+ years now. no employees, no funding, no board meetings." In the same post he summarises the operating doctrine he has repeated for years: pick a problem you personally have, ship "something ugly in a weekend," and "charge money from day 1."</p>
<p>His site nav lists a set of projects: stats, projects, api, investments. That structure matters more than any single product. He treats the business as a portfolio of small software properties under one owner, with shared infrastructure and a shared audience, rather than as one company with a roadmap.</p>

<h2>What the Products Actually Are</h2>
<p><strong>Nomads.com.</strong> The homepage brands it as the "#1 Nomad Community Since 2014" and lists concrete features: city pages with scores (nomad, solomad, promad, family, quality of life), trips, chat, a members map, a dating app, a friend finder, meetups (196 per year in 100+ cities, per the page), tools like NomadGPT, an MCP server, an API, a FIRE calculator, a climate finder, and the "State of remote work" research. It also shows live city counters such as "Bangkok Nov 11 – Dec 21, 55,581+ going," which is a demand signal but not a revenue figure.</p>
<p><strong>Remote OK.</strong> A job board. The FAQ describes the flow precisely: post a job at remoteok.com/post-a-job, pay at checkout, and the post goes live immediately and is "distributed through our network of 100s of job boards that use our API to index our jobs, and broadcasted to millions of remote workers via email and on the site." Every paid post carries a unique edit link that allows editing the title, description, salary, logo, invoice/VAT details and apply URL, and allows closing the post.</p>

<h2>How He Gets Customers</h2>
<p>This is the part most readers want, and it is also the part where the public record is thinnest. The reviewed sources do not describe a paid acquisition budget, an SEO playbook, or a specific conversion funnel. What they do document:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Building in public on X.</strong> His February 2026 post states outright: "grow on Twitter/X by building in public. your journey IS the marketing." That is his stated method, in his own words, not an inference.</li>
<li><strong>Distribution baked into the product.</strong> Remote OK's FAQ says paid job posts are automatically syndicated to hundreds of job boards via its API and pushed to email subscribers. A single paying employer therefore reaches audiences he does not own. The FAQ does not state subscriber counts.</li>
<li><strong>A long-lived, search-and-community-shaped property.</strong> Nomads.com has been running since 2014 per its own homepage, and its city pages, meetups and chat give new visitors a reason to return beyond a one-time read. The reviewed sources do not attribute this to any specific SEO tactic.</li>
<li><strong>An owned audience of readers.</strong> His posts carry a subscribe prompt referencing "187,008 people read my posts in full by email." That is a distribution asset he created through writing, and it is stated on the page.</li>
</ul>
<p>Treat the rest as analysis rather than reportage: a job board and a community site in the same niche can plausibly feed each other, but none of the reviewed documents establishes that Nomads.com and Remote OK cross-promote or share an audience. Do not assume it.</p>

<h2>The Money: Revenue, Costs and Margin</h2>
<p>The headline number in his 23 September 2026 post is "$10M/y in revenue and investment gains with a 94.5% profit margin." Read it carefully. It is revenue <em>plus</em> investment gains, not revenue alone. He separates the two within the same post: "My business revenue has remained very stable at about $200K-$250K/mo, but even with that investment returns have now surpassed that." He also flags the caveat himself: "Investments fluctuate a lot… and most investment gains are unrealized (I never sell!)."</p>
<p>On costs, his 3 November 2026 post is unusually specific. He reports total business expenses of about $300 per month for the sites excluding Photo AI, serving "20,000 paying customers," "252 million visitors," "2.4 billion requests" and "2.4 petabyte (PB) in bandwidth." Photo AI is broken out because of scale: a $7,000/month GPU bill plus about $800/month for Cloudflare R2 storage, which he says would be roughly $8,000/month if added to the base figure. He also lists the savings that got him there: GPU spend cut from $36,000/mo to $7,000/mo, about $3,000/mo by consolidating servers from Digital Ocean and Linode onto Hetzner, a backup bill falling from $1,200/mo to $26/mo after capping versioning at 30 days, R2 dropping from $1,200/mo to $800/mo via lifecycle rules, $118/mo of unused domain renewals cut, and $100/mo by downgrading a Claude plan he wasn't fully using. He claims a 90% cost cut year over year.</p>
<p>None of these numbers are audited, and the $300 figure deliberately excludes his most expensive project.</p>

<h2>Starting Costs and Scale, as Disclosed</h2>
<p>Starting costs for Nomads.com or Remote OK are not disclosed in any reviewed source. The only cost guidance he gives is general and applies to his method, not to a specific launch: in the February 2026 post, "host on cheap VPS ($5-20/mo). not AWS. you don't need kubernetes for 1,000 users," and "use boring tech. PHP, SQLite, vanilla JS."</p>
<p>The scale figures that are documented are the paying-customer and traffic counts above, the 2014 launch year on the Nomads.com homepage, and the Remote OK FAQ's description of a syndication network of "100s of job boards." Numbers for headcount are clear: zero employees, stated repeatedly by the founder.</p>

<h2>What You Can Actually Copy</h2>
<p>These are operating principles, labelled here as analysis drawn from his published statements:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Sell before you polish.</strong> Charging from day one forces a real signal. His words, not a paraphrase.</li>
<li><strong>Keep infrastructure boring and cheap.</strong> A $5–20 VPS is a stated preference; the Hetzner consolidation shows the same instinct applied at scale.</li>
<li><strong>Audit recurring spend quarterly.</strong> The backup bill going from $1,200 to $26 a month for the same job is the single most transferable item in his cost post.</li>
<li><strong>Build distribution into the product.</strong> If your product can syndicate itself — an API, a feed, an email list — each paying customer brings reach you don't pay for.</li>
<li><strong>Publish your numbers.</strong> His revenue posts are themselves marketing: 855,555 views and 8,232 likes on one post, per the page.</li>
<li><strong>Stay a one-person company deliberately.</strong> He argues for "ramen profitable > series A" and says no to investors and cofounders.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What doesn't work</h2>
<p>The failure modes around this kind of business are well-documented in the numbers above, even if he doesn't dwell on them.</p>
<p><strong>Counting investment gains as business revenue.</strong> His own $10M headline mixes the two, and he immediately warns that most gains are unrealized and could reverse in a bad year. Anyone quoting "$10M one-person business" as operating revenue is misreading the post. The operating figure he gives is $200K–$250K a month.</p>
<p><strong>Letting costs creep.</strong> His expense post is essentially a confession: a decade of file versions pushed a backup bill to $1,200 a month, an unmaintained video pipeline left GPU spend at $36,000 a month, and unused domains renewed for years. The savings came from auditing, not from a new idea. Businesses that don't audit quietly bleed the margin that makes solo operation viable.</p>
<p><strong>Copying the launch, not the decade.</strong> Nomads.com has been live since 2014. The revenue is a product of compounding time plus an accumulated audience, and the reviewed sources give no formula for shortcutting that.</p>
<p><strong>Treating "free users" as a growth engine.</strong> His stated position is blunt: "free users give you nothing but support tickets." That's a strong claim, not a law, but it explains why both products monetize from the start.</p>
<p><strong>Assuming you know the pricing.</strong> Neither Remote OK's posting prices nor Nomads.com's membership prices appear in the materials reviewed here. Anyone building a copycat business on a guessed price point is working from nothing.</p>
