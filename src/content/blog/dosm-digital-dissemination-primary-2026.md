## What happened

The Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) will implement digital dissemination of publications as the primary approach effective 1 June 2026. The announcement appears on the DOSM website, retrieved on 10 November 2026. The change means printed publications will no longer be the default format for official statistics; digital downloads and online access become the primary channel.

DOSM is the government agency responsible for producing Malaysia's official statistics, including population counts, economic indicators, trade figures and vital statistics such as births and deaths. Its publications feed into government policy, business planning, academic research and media reporting.

> **Digital dissemination:** the practice of distributing publications through digital channels such as website downloads, online databases and electronic files, rather than printing and distributing physical copies. Under the new DOSM approach, digital becomes the primary method, with print no longer the default.

The shift does not change the data itself. DOSM continues to publish the same statistics; what changes is the delivery format. A researcher who once received a printed volume will now download a file. A student who once consulted a library copy will now access the same tables online.

## Why now

The move follows a two-to-three-year thread of government agencies shifting services online. DOSM has progressively expanded its digital offerings, including online databases and downloadable datasets, while reducing the prominence of printed volumes. The 1 June 2026 effective date formalises a direction that has been building.

The second reason is cost. Printing, warehousing and distributing physical publications carries ongoing expense. Digital dissemination removes those costs. For a government agency under budget pressure, the shift is a way to deliver the same information at lower cost.

The third reason is usage patterns. Researchers, students and businesses increasingly expect data in digital form, where it can be searched, copied and analysed. A printed volume requires manual transcription; a digital file can be loaded directly into a spreadsheet. The change aligns DOSM's delivery with how its users already work.

## So what

The gain lands on users who already work digitally. A student writing a thesis can download a dataset and analyse it directly. A business planning a budget can pull the latest figures into a spreadsheet without retyping. The mechanism is efficiency: digital files move from source to analysis in seconds, where printed volumes required manual entry.

The cost lands on users without reliable internet access or digital skills. A small business owner in a rural area, a student without a computer, or a researcher accustomed to printed reference copies faces a steeper path to the same data. The shift assumes connectivity and digital literacy that not every user has.

The third actor is the library. University and public libraries that once held printed DOSM volumes will need to ensure their users can access digital versions. That means terminals, internet access and staff who can guide users to the right files. The burden of access shifts from the publisher to the reader's environment.

## What it means for Malaysia

The consequence lands on Malaysian students, researchers and small businesses who use DOSM data. For a student writing a final-year project, the change means learning to find, download and cite digital datasets rather than photocopying printed tables. ## FAQ

**Will printed DOSM publications disappear entirely after 1 June 2026?**

The announcement states that digital dissemination becomes the primary approach, not that print is eliminated. The word "primary" indicates a change in priority, not a complete ban. Users should expect digital to be the default channel, but the announcement does not say printed copies will be unavailable. The practical effect is that users who want print will need to check whether it remains available on request or through libraries. The safest assumption is that digital becomes the standard route, and users should plan their access accordingly. The announcement does not specify the fate of existing printed stock or whether print-on-demand remains an option.

**How does this affect the data I already use for my studies or business?**

The data itself does not change. DOSM continues to publish the same statistics; the shift affects the delivery format. A student who used printed volumes will now download files. A business that kept a printed reference will now bookmark a web page. The practical change is in how you access the numbers, not in what the numbers say. The Vital Statistics Time Series, for example, remains the same dataset whether you read it in print or on screen. The change affects your workflow: downloading, storing and citing digital files becomes the standard practice.

**What should I do now to prepare for the change?**

The practical step is to familiarise yourself with DOSM's digital platforms before 1 June 2026. Check the DOSM website, learn where publications are posted, and test downloading a dataset. For students, this means practising citing digital files with retrieval dates. For businesses, it means identifying which statistics you use regularly and bookmarking the relevant pages. The change rewards users who are already comfortable with digital access and penalises those who are not, so the preparation is about building that comfort before the effective date.

