## What happened

The Cabinet decided on 6 November 2026 that every Malaysian family that lost a relative in the Nepal-Tibet flood will receive a one-off payment of RM10,000. Human Resources Minister R. Ramanan announced the decision, as reported by Malay Mail on the same day. The money is paid through the National Disaster Management Agency, known as Nadma — the federal agency that coordinates disaster response and relief in Malaysia.

The payment covers all 54 Malaysian victims of the flood and debris flow that struck on 26 August 2026. The disaster began with a glacial ice and rock avalanche in the upper catchment area of Lhende Khola in Tibet, which sent water and debris downstream. As of 6 November 2026, the flood has claimed 1,451 lives. About 5,700 individuals are still reported missing, including more than 600 foreign nationals.

> **Nadma:** the National Disaster Management Agency, the federal body that coordinates disaster response, relief and aid distribution in Malaysia. When the government pays disaster compensation, Nadma is the agency that runs the payment.

The payment order matters as much as the amount. Where a victim left a spouse, the spouse receives the payment first. Where there is no spouse, the parents come next. Where there is neither spouse nor parents, the children receive it. This is the sequence the government set for distributing the RM10,000 to each family.

## Why now

The flood happened on 26 August 2026. The payment decision came on 6 November 2026 — more than two months later. That gap is the thread.

In the weeks after the disaster, the priority was rescue and identification. A payment cannot be made to a family until the victim is confirmed. With about 5,700 people still reported missing as of 6 November, identification is still incomplete for many families in the region. The 54 Malaysian victims are the confirmed Malaysian dead, and the payment is tied to that confirmed list.

The second reason the decision surfaces this month is the Cabinet calendar. A one-off payment of this kind requires a Cabinet decision, and the Prime Minister made the call. Once the Cabinet decides, the announcement follows. The mechanism is a government payment cycle: confirm the victims, set the amount, set the order of payment, then release through Nadma.

The third reason is the missing-persons figure. With more than 600 foreign nationals among the roughly 5,700 still missing, the disaster remains an active international recovery operation. Malaysia's decision to pay its own victims' families is a national response inside a much larger and still unresolved event.

## So what

The gain lands on 54 Malaysian families. Each receives RM10,000, a fixed sum that does not vary with the victim's income, age or dependants. The payment order decides who inside the family actually holds the money: the spouse first, then the parents, then the children. That order prevents a dispute between relatives over who is entitled, because the sequence is set before anyone applies.

The cost lands on the public purse, and it is small. Fifty-four families at RM10,000 each is RM540,000. That is the total commitment to Malaysian families under this decision. The larger cost of the disaster sits elsewhere: 1,451 lives lost and about 5,700 people still missing across the region.

The third actor is the family with no confirmed death. A family whose relative is among the roughly 5,700 still missing is not covered by this payment, because the payment is tied to the 54 confirmed Malaysian victims. For those families, the wait continues, and the decision does not say what happens if a missing person is later confirmed dead. That is the open question the announcement leaves.
