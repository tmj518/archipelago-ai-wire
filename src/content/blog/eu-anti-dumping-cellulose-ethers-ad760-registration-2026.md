## What happened

The European Commission opened an anti-dumping investigation into certain cellulose ethers from
China and the Republic of Korea on 24 September 2026, under Article 5 of Regulation (EU) 2016/1036.
The complaint came from Ashland Industries Nederland BV, Ashland Specialties Belgium BV and SE Tylose
GmbH & Co. KG, and was lodged on 10 August 2026.

The product is defined by chemistry rather than by use. Six cellulose ethers are in scope, named by
CAS number: methyl cellulose, hydroxyethyl cellulose, hydroxyethyl methyl cellulose, hydroxypropyl
methyl cellulose, ethyl hydroxyethyl cellulose and methyl ethyl hydroxyethyl cellulose. The notice
adds "regardless of colour, physical form, or grade" — so viscosity grade, which is what a buyer
usually specifies, does not take a consignment out of scope.

The tariff entry is CN ex 3912 39 85, with three TARIC codes: 3912 39 85 30, 3912 39 85 60 and
3912 39 85 90.

## Why now

Three clocks started on 24 September 2026 and they run at different speeds.

The fast one has already stopped. Comment on the product scope closed ten days after publication, on
4 October 2026. Hearing requests and comment on provisional measures closed fifteen days after
publication, on 9 October 2026. If you import cellulose ethers and wanted to argue that your grade
is not the product under investigation, that argument had a deadline and it passed.

The slow one is the one that costs money. Under Article 7(1) of the basic Regulation, provisional
measures can be imposed no earlier than 60 days after initiation and no later than seven months —
eight at the outside. From 24 September 2026, seven months is 24 April 2027 and eight months is
24 May 2027. Under Article 6(9) the investigation concludes within a year, and not more than fourteen
months: 24 September 2027, or 24 November 2027 at the outside.

The third clock has no published start date at all, and it is the one to watch. The registration
regulation will be published "in due time", and the notice says "at an early stage". Between the
closed comment window on 4 October 2026 and the earliest possible provisional duty on 24 April 2027
there are 190 days in which registration can be ordered while nothing is payable.

## So what

Registration is the cheapest tool the Commission has, and that is exactly why it is the one to plan
for. A registration regulation imposes no duty and raises no revenue question, so it can be adopted
early without the evidential work that a provisional duty needs. What it does is create a
retrospective liability whose size is unknown at the moment the goods clear.

The mechanics matter for anyone modelling landed cost. An ad valorem anti-dumping duty is charged on
the CIF value of the goods, and retroactive collection reaches the consignments that were registered.
So the exposure is not a share of your margin. It is a percentage of what you paid for the goods,
applied to every entered consignment inside the registration window.

Take one 20-foot container holding 18 tonnes of cellulose ethers at a stated CIF value of 50,000
dollars, which is 2.78 dollars a kilogram, and apply the range:

| Rate on CIF value | Duty on the container | What it does to a 15 percent gross margin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0% | $0 | nothing — the case closes without measures |
| 10% | $5,000 | takes about a third of the gross margin |
| 25% | $12,500 | takes most of it |
| 50% | $25,000 | exceeds the gross margin on the container |

The 50,000 dollars is a stated input, not a market price. The point of the table is the shape rather
than the numbers: the top row and the bottom row differ by 25,000 dollars on a single container, and
the decision between them is made inside the Commission, not in your negotiation.

## For you

- **Buyer with a landed-cost model already:** add a line for a registered-import provision at
  whichever rate you carry for contingency, and set it against CIF value rather than unit price,
  because CIF is the base the duty uses. The number to carry is the CIF exposure, not the

  index](/blog/freight-index-linked-contract-two-errors-2026/).
- **Buyer without one:** the useful question is not whether a duty arrives but how much of your next
  twelve months of CIF value sits inside the registration window. If registration is ordered in
  January 2027 and definitive measures land in November 2027, then ten months of your intake is
  registered. [The landed-cost method](/blog/importing-from-china-documents-checklist/) shows where
  to put the line.
- **Anyone holding stock:** registered imports are identified by customs entry, so the consignments
  carrying the contingent liability are the ones entered inside the window, whether or not you still
  hold them. For how the EU charges duty per item rather than per consignment, see [the EU per-item
  customs charge](/blog/eu-per-item-customs-charge-2026/).
- **Exporters and their agents:** the notice defines the product by CAS number across six ethers and
  then removes grade as a distinguishing feature, so a specification argument is a product-scope
  argument and the scope window closed on 4 October 2026. The question worth answering early is
  whether the exporting entity is likely to be sampled, because an individual margin follows from the
  sample and the duty that attaches to your customer follows from the margin.

## FAQ

### Is there a duty on Chinese cellulose ethers now?

No. On the date of writing no anti-dumping duty applies to cellulose ethers from China or Korea. What
exists is an investigation opened on 24 September 2026 and a stated intention to register imports.
Under Article 7(1) of Regulation (EU) 2016/1036 a provisional duty cannot be imposed earlier than 60
days after initiation, and the notice puts the outer limit at eight months, which is 24 May 2027. So
a duty is possible from late April 2027 and impossible before then.

### What does registration actually change for me?

It creates a contingent liability on consignments that enter after the registration regulation is
published. You pay nothing at the border. If definitive duties are later imposed, the Commission can
collect them retroactively on the registered imports, which means a bill arriving after the goods
have been sold. The size of that bill depends on a rate that has not been calculated yet, so
registration converts a known cost into an unknown one without changing the amount payable today.

### Can I avoid the exposure by changing my supplier?

Partly, and only for the country dimension. The case covers the People's Republic of China and the
Republic of Korea. A supplier in a third country is outside the scope of this investigation, but two
things decide whether that helps: whether the goods are actually originating in a third country under
the non-preferential origin rules, and whether a separate case covers them. Buyers should also expect
the registration obligation to attach to the importer of record rather than to the exporter, which is
where the liability sits in practice.

### Does my viscosity grade fall inside the product scope?

The notice defines the product by CAS number and then says "regardless of colour, physical form, or
grade". Viscosity grade is a specification of the same chemical, so it does not take a consignment
out of scope on the face of the notice. The window to argue about product scope closed on 4 October
2026. An argument that a particular grade is a different product is now something to raise through
the file rather than through the scope consultation.

### When does this end?

Under Article 6(9) the investigation concludes within one year of publication, and not more than
fourteen months. From 24 September 2026 that is 24 September 2027, or 24 November 2027 at the
outside. The notice also sets out the standard sequence: information on planned provisional duties
four weeks before they are imposed, three working days to comment on the accuracy of the
calculations, and definitive measures at the end. The register entry for AD760 shows definitive
measures expected on 20 November 2027.

## How we calculated this

Two calculations, both stated so they can be redone.

```text
duty on a consignment = CIF value x rate

stated container: 18,000 kg at 50,000 dollars CIF
  = 2.78 dollars per kg

at each rate, CIF value 50,000 dollars:
   0%  = 0
  10%  = 5,000
  25%  = 12,500
  50%  = 25,000

range = 25,000 - 0 = 25,000 dollars per container

registration window in which duty can be ordered
while nothing is payable:
  4 October 2026  ->  24 April 2027
  = 202 days
```

The duty figures convert a percentage into a bill. An ad valorem anti-dumping duty is charged on the
CIF value entered, so the arithmetic is CIF value multiplied by the rate. The container is stated at
50,000 dollars CIF and 18 tonnes net, which is 2.78 dollars a kilogram — a reader input, not a
published price. The rate range is 0 to 50 percent. The table reports the four rates rather than a
single expected one, because the Commission has published no rate and any single number would be
invented.

The 190-day figure is the gap between two dates in the notice: 4 October 2026, which is ten days
after publication and the close of the product-scope window, and 24 April 2027, which is seven months
after publication and the earliest date Article 7(1) allows for provisional measures. It measures the
period in which registration can be ordered while no duty is payable.

## The point

The question importers are asking about AD760 is what the duty will be. That question cannot be
answered, and the more useful one is when the liability starts. The Commission has told you: it
intends to register imports early, and registration is what makes a later duty reach backwards. On
one 20-foot container the difference between no duty and a 50 percent duty is 25,000 dollars, and
that difference is created by a regulation that can be published any day between now and 24 April
2027 without a single euro becoming payable at the time.
