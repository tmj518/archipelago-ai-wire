## What happened

Three Singapore banks have moved, or are moving, into selling physical gold to ordinary customers rather than only to institutions and the wealthy.

DBS announced in June that it would offer physical gold tokens to retail customers through its digibank app in the second half of 2026. A token here means a digital record of a claim on a specific quantity of metal held by the bank, bought and sold inside the bank's app rather than delivered as a bar.

Also from June, OCBC's institutional clients and the high-net-worth and ultra-high-net-worth clients of its private banking arm, Bank of Singapore, can buy and store physical gold bars with the bank. That is a narrower group than DBS's retail app customers, and the distinction matters for a Malaysian reader: OCBC's June step reached institutions and wealthy private-banking clients, not the general public.

UOB was previously the only bank in Singapore offering physical gold products to retail investors. That single-supplier position is what the DBS and OCBC moves end.

The context for all three is the price. Demand for the metal pushed gold to an all-time high of US$5,600 per ounce in January 2026, according to the CNA article retrieved on 23 November 2026. Every figure in this piece comes from that article and the bank announcements it reports.

> **Physical gold:** metal you own, or hold a claim on, rather than a contract whose value follows the gold price. A gold bar in a vault, a token backed by a specific bar, and a gold savings account are three different things. The first two carry a claim on metal; the third is a promise from a financial institution. The difference shows up when you try to take delivery, or when the institution fails.

## Why now

The thread runs through 2026 in three steps. In January, gold set an all-time high of US$5,600 an ounce. In June, two banks moved: DBS announced a retail token product for the second half of the year, and OCBC opened physical bar buying and storage to institutions and private-banking clients. The CNA analysis that pulls the three together was published on 24 November 2026, which is why the story surfaces now rather than in June.

The mechanism behind the banks' behaviour is demand. A price at an all-time high is a signal that customers want the metal. A bank that does not sell it loses the transaction to one that does, or to a gold dealer outside the banking system. UOB held the retail physical-gold business alone; DBS and OCBC are closing that gap. This is competition for a customer relationship, not a charitable widening of access.

The second thread is distribution. DBS is putting the product inside its digibank app, which is the channel an ordinary customer already uses for transfers and payments. Selling gold through an app lowers the cost of reaching a small buyer: no branch visit, no counter staff, no minimum bar size. That is the part that turns a wealthy-client product into a retail one.

## So what

The gain lands on the small buyer in Singapore and, indirectly, on the Malaysian who already holds a Singapore bank account. Buying a token in an app is cheaper and easier than buying a bar, storing it, and finding a dealer when you want to sell. The spread between the buying price and the selling price is where the cost sits, and an app product compresses the handling cost that a physical bar carries.

The cost lands on anyone who reads "physical gold" as "safe". Gold has no yield. It pays no interest and no dividend. Its price is set by demand, and demand pushed it to US$5,600 an ounce in January 2026. A buyer who enters at a record price is buying an asset whose main risk is the price itself. The CNA analysis makes volatility the headline risk, and that is the correct framing.

The third actor is the incumbent, UOB. A bank that held a retail monopoly now faces two competitors, one of them distributing through an app. The likely response is a lower spread, a smaller minimum purchase, or a bundled product. That response is a scenario, not a fact: no bank has published its pricing for the new products in the material here.

## FAQ

**Is buying gold through a Singapore bank app the same as owning a gold bar?**

No. A token bought in DBS's digibank app is a digital record of a claim on metal the bank holds, not a bar in your hand. The difference appears when you want delivery, when you want to sell, and when the institution itself runs into trouble. A bar you store yourself carries storage and insurance costs and a dealer's spread when you sell. A token carries the bank's terms, which the source material does not set out. Ask the bank in writing what happens on delivery and on closure of the account before buying.

**Why did gold reach US$5,600 an ounce in January 2026?**

The CNA article attributes the all-time high to demand for the metal. It does not break that demand into its components, and this article does not either. What the figure establishes is the level a new buyer is entering against. A price at a record is a statement about what buyers were willing to pay in January 2026, not a promise about what they will pay next year. The same demand that lifted the price can reverse, and the CNA analysis names volatility as the main risk.

**Does this change anything for a Malaysian who buys gold locally?**

Not directly. A Malaysian buying from a local dealer pays that dealer's spread and, on jewellery, a making charge. Neither moves because a Singapore bank launched an app. The change is a new comparison point for Malaysians who already hold a Singapore bank account, and a signal that banks expect retail demand for the metal to continue. For everyone else, the practical step is unchanged: ask for the buy-back price before you buy, and treat the metal's price as the risk it is.
