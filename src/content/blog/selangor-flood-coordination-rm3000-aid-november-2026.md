## What happened

The Prime Minister, Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim, chaired a special meeting on Selangor flood disaster coordination at Parliament House on 12 November 2026. Malay Mail reported the meeting the same day, quoting the Selangor Menteri Besar, Datuk Seri Amirudin Shari.

The meeting focused on four key actions. Two are named in the report: strengthening early warning systems with the Malaysian Meteorological Department (MetMalaysia), and improving disaster management coordination by the Selangor State Disaster Management Committee. The report names the agencies involved in the response: MetMalaysia, the Selangor State Disaster Management Committee, the Drainage and Irrigation Department (DID), Kuala Lumpur City Hall (DBKL), the National Disaster Management Agency (NADMA), the Public Works Department (JKR) and the Selangor Implementation Coordination Unit (ICU).

Alongside the meeting, the state is channelling the Selangor People's Flood Disaster Special Aid totalling RM3,000 to each eligible head of household, subject to set conditions. The report does not list those conditions.

The geography is specific. Special attention is being given to flood-prone areas, particularly the Petaling and Kuala Selangor districts, which recorded 1,180 evacuees at seven temporary relief centres as of 12 November 2026.

> **Temporary relief centre:** a school, hall or community building opened by the authorities to shelter people who have had to leave their homes during a flood. The count of evacuees at these centres is the standard public measure of how many people a flood has displaced at a given moment.

## Why now

Selangor's flooding is not new. The thread runs through repeated flood seasons in the same districts, and the same set of agencies appearing at each response. What changed this month is the level at which the response is being coordinated. A meeting chaired by the Prime Minister turns a state-level operation into a federal one, and it puts the warning system and the drainage network on the same agenda as relief.

The second reason is that the numbers are live. The 1,180 evacuees at seven centres are people who had left their homes as of 12 November 2026. A coordination meeting held while relief centres are open is a meeting under pressure, not a post-season review.

The third reason is institutional. The report names seven agencies across federal, state and city level. Flood response in the Klang Valley crosses all of them: MetMalaysia issues warnings, DID manages rivers and drains, DBKL manages city drains, NADMA coordinates disaster response, JKR manages roads and infrastructure, and the state committee and ICU coordinate on the ground. A meeting that agrees four key actions is an attempt to make those agencies move on one timetable.

## So what

The gain lands on households in Petaling and Kuala Selangor who get warning time. An early warning system that works is the cheapest form of flood protection there is: it does not stop the water, but it lets a family move a car, lift documents and leave before the road closes. The mechanism is time. Every extra hour of warning converts a rescue into an evacuation.

The cost lands on the same households in a different way. A coordination meeting and an aid announcement do not drain a neighbourhood. The drainage upgrade order is an instruction to implement, and implementation runs through budgets, procurement and construction schedules that the report does not describe. Until the drains are widened and the pumps are installed, the flood risk in Petaling and Kuala Selangor is unchanged.

The third actor is the state government. It carries the aid bill and the coordination role at the same time. The RM3,000 payout is a direct transfer to households, and the conditions attached to it decide who receives it. The report states that conditions exist but does not state what they are, which leaves the eligibility question open for the reader.

**Which agency is now responsible for flood warnings in Selangor?**

Coordination of disaster management sits with the Selangor State Disaster Management Committee. The response also involves DID, DBKL, NADMA, JKR and the Selangor ICU. **Is the drainage problem fixed?**

A drainage upgrade is an instruction to implement, and implementation runs through budgets, procurement and construction. The 1,180 evacuees at seven relief centres as of 12 November 2026 show that the water arrived before the works did. Residents in Petaling and Kuala Selangor should treat the plan as a direction of travel, not a completed fix.
