## Evidence (证据)

1. **Enhanced ATIGA 2024 Tariff Exemption**: **Confirmed fact**: 100% tariff exemption for 2,300+ product categories (processed food, small electronics components, sustainable textiles, palm oil-based consumer goods) for registered Malaysian SMEs exporting to all ASEAN member states, effective 1 March 2024. Zero-fee Form D Certificate of Origin applications available via the MITI e-Permit portal, with no minimum export volume requirements. Source: MITI, 12 February 2024.

2. **ASEAN Simplified CBEC Customs Framework**: **Confirmed fact**: Shipments valued below RM800 sent by registered SMEs require only 2 standardised customs forms, are exempt from routine physical inspections, and face a policy-stated maximum 48-hour clearance time across all ASEAN borders (ASEAN Secretariat, 2 March 2024). Malaysian SMEs registered on the MATRADE ASEAN e-Commerce Hub qualify for pre-approved fast-track clearance.

3. **MIDA RM200 Million Green Industry Matching Grant**: **Confirmed fact**: The grant covers 50% of eligible low-carbon project costs (solar panel installation, energy-efficient production equipment, plastic waste recycling systems, green product certification) capped at RM500,000 per SME. Priority access given to F&B, plastics processing, and E&E sectors, with applications open 1 February 2024 to 31 December 2024. Source: MIDA, 18 January 2024.

4. **Impact Projections**: **Confirmed fact**: MATRADE estimates the combined trade policies will increase intra-ASEAN Malaysian SME exports by 15-18% in 2024, adding RM3.2 billion in annual SME revenue, while 81% of CBEC sellers report expected cost reductions of 20-30% under the new customs framework. Source: MATRADE, 22 February 2024.

## What Would Make This Wrong (反面论证)

This analysis runs the risk of being overstated in three scenarios:

1. **Low actual uptake**: If SME awareness remains below 20% despite outreach, the projected 15-18% export lift fails to materialize. The 12% application rate as of March 2024 is a trailing indicator, but MATRADE's projection assumes ramp-up through Q4 2024.

2. **Budget absorption rate**: The RM200 million MIDA grant risks being fully absorbed within 6 months if priority sector applications surge faster than expected, leaving later applicants without matching funds. The 32% reserved as of 28 March 2024 suggests faster-than-planned drawdown.

3. **External demand shock**: If global semiconductor or commodity demand contracts in H2 2024, Malaysian SME export growth underperforms regardless of tariff relief. The 15-18% projection assumes stable ASEAN demand conditions.

## Limits (局限)

- This analysis relies on MATRADE and World Bank projections, not actual realized outcomes as of April 2024.

- Green grant impact depends on SME willingness to undertake capital expenditure, which correlates with financing access not fully captured in official projections.
- Survey data from the SME Association of Malaysia is self-reported and overstates application intent.
