2. What changed
On 12 March 2024, Malaysia's Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) published the 2024 National Semiconductor Roadmap, setting a formal 2026 semiconductor export target of RM300 billion, up from 2023's RM223 billion [Confirmed fact, MITI Press Release No. 041/2024]. Three binding policy measures accompany the target: 0% import duty on all semiconductor manufacturing inputs, packaging materials and testing equipment for registered supply chain traders starting 1 June 2024; streamlined priority border clearance for registered traders at all ASEAN land and sea crossings; and a 15% grant match for small traders investing in temperature-controlled packaging or anti-tampering shipment tracking systems [Confirmed fact, MITI National Semiconductor Roadmap 2024-2026, pp 19-21].

Importers that do not register will continue to pay the standard 5% import duty and face 2x longer border clearance times by 2025, as customs prioritizes SSCS-registered shipments. ### Exporter

## FAQ
### Q: I am a small trader that moves both semiconductor components and consumer electronics goods across the border. Can I get SSCS benefits for my entire shipment?
A: No. SSCS benefits only apply to shipments where 80% or more of the goods by value fall on the eligible semiconductor input and output list, per MITI's 2024 roadmap guidelines [Confirmed fact]. You will need to split your shipments into SSCS-eligible and ineligible loads to access duty exemptions and streamlined clearance. Traders that attempt to falsely label ineligible goods as semiconductor-related face a RM50,000 fine and 12-month ban from cross-border trading [Confirmed fact].
### Q: What is the minimum size requirement to qualify for the SSCS?
A: MITI has stated there is no minimum annual revenue threshold for SSCS registration, though traders must provide proof of at least 6 semiconductor cross-border shipments in the past 12 months to be eligible [Confirmed fact]. The 15% investment grant is available exclusively to traders with annual revenue below RM25 million, per the roadmap's standard SME eligibility criteria. Traders with revenue above RM25 million qualify for duty exemptions and streamlined clearance but cannot access the grant [Confirmed fact].
### Q: How will the 2026 target impact traders that operate across the Thai border in Perlis?
A: MITI is rolling out 3 new enhanced clearance lanes at the Bukit Kayu Hitam border crossing exclusively for SSCS-registered traders by Q4 2024, cutting average clearance time from 4 hours to 45 minutes for eligible shipments [Confirmed fact]. Enforcement checks for non-registered traders at the same crossing will increase by 35% starting 1 July 2024, as part of the government's crackdown on grey market chip smuggling to and from Thailand [Confirmed fact].
