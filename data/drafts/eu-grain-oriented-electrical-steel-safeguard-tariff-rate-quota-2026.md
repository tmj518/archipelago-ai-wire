## Why now

The date is the operative fact. Any entry of grain-oriented electrical steel on or after 18 September 2026 falls under the measure. Goods already in transit, or on order with a delivery date after that point, are inside it.

The two measure types named are the reason this is not a simple duty question. A safeguard built on tariff-rate quotas and price thresholds is designed so that some volume enters at a low or nil rate and the cost concentrates on volume above a line, or on goods entered below a price floor. For a product bought on annual contracts, that means the exposure is not spread evenly across the year — it depends on where in the quota period your entry falls and at what price it is declared.

## So what

The two instruments test different things, and an importer has to test both.

**Tariff-rate quota — a cliff, not a slope.** Volume up to a set quantity enters at the in-quota rate; volume above it enters at the out-of-quota rate. Your cost consequence is the gap between those two rates multiplied by the volume above the line. If the quota is administered first-come-first-served, the line can be crossed before your vessel arrives, and goods you priced at the in-quota rate are entered at the out-of-quota rate. If it is allocated by country or supplier, the line is fixed to your origin.

**Price threshold — a floor under the invoice.** This tests the price at which goods are entered, not the volume. Goods entered below the threshold are treated differently from goods at or above it. A cheap purchase price is therefore not automatically a cheap landed cost: the threshold, not the invoice, sets the base. A shipment can be inside the quota by volume and still be caught by the price test, or the reverse.

**Does a tariff-rate quota mean I pay duty on everything?**
No. A tariff-rate quota splits treatment by volume: the quantity inside the quota enters at the in-quota rate, and only volume above that line enters at the out-of-quota rate. Your cost is the difference between the two rates applied to the volume above the line. The practical risk is timing. If the quota is administered first-come-first-served, the line can be crossed before your goods are entered, and a shipment priced at the in-quota rate is assessed at the out-of-quota rate. Ask your broker for the remaining balance before you commit to a delivery date.

**How is a price threshold different from a duty rate?**
A price threshold tests the price at which goods are entered rather than the volume. Goods entered below the threshold are treated differently from goods entered at or above it. The consequence for a buyer is that a low purchase price does not guarantee a low landed cost: the threshold, not the invoice, sets the base. A shipment can be inside the quota by volume and still be caught by the price test. Where the declared value comes from a related-party or transfer-priced purchase, the threshold is the number that will be argued about at entry.
