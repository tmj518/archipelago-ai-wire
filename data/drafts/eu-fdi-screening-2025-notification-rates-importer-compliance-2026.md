## What happened

The European Commission Directorate-General for Trade and Economic Security released its sixth annual FDI screening report on 14 October 2026, covering full 2025 operational data for the EU-wide FDI cooperation mechanism. The report confirms a 40% year-on-year increase in FDI notifications from 2024 to 2025, the highest annual rise since the mechanism was first introduced. Total 2025 notifications reached 667, up from 477 in 2024.

Of those 667 2025 cases, 88% were fully closed within 15 days of submission. The remaining 12% of notified investments required an in-depth security risk assessment, a 4 percentage point rise over the 2024 share of cases that moved to full review. Across the three consecutive reporting years 2023, 2024 and 2025, the Commission issued a formal opinion in less than 2% of all notified cases.

The five sectors with the highest volume of 2025 notifications are manufacturing at 27% of all transactions, information and communication technologies at 19%, financial activities at 17%, wholesale and retail at 13%, and professional activities at 8%. The report also confirms that the new EU Foreign Investment Screening Regulation (FISR), adopted 17 June 2026, will apply in full from 17 January 2028.

> **EU FDI screening cooperation mechanism** — the cross-border administrative framework that allows EU Member States and the European Commission to review foreign direct investments entering the single market for potential risks to security, public order, and critical infrastructure. Notifications are submitted to the relevant national screening authority, which then shares relevant details with the Commission and other Member States for coordinated review.

## FAQ
**What is the minimum timeline I should plan for a low-risk FDI notification in 2026?**
For a low-risk investment in the wholesale and retail or financial activities sectors, where 92% to 94% of cases close within 15 days, you can plan for a 15-business-day review window for a complete, fully documented submission. Add a 5-calendar-day buffer for administrative processing at the national screening authority, and you can schedule your investment closing for 25 calendar days after you submit your notification. This timeline is consistent with 2025 operational data, and applies to all investments that do not touch critical infrastructure, sensitive technologies, or public security assets. You do not need to build a multi-month buffer for standard low-risk transactions, as the 2025 data confirms the vast majority of these cases clear quickly.

**How will the 2028 FISR rules change my notification requirements as a third-country investor?**
The new FISR rules taking effect 17 January 2028 will create a unified EU-wide notification form, harmonize review timelines across all Member States, and expand the scope of mandatory notification to cover additional categories of investment in critical raw materials, advanced semiconductors, and health security assets. The 2025 baseline data on notification volumes and closure rates gives you a clear performance benchmark to measure the new system against, as the Commission has stated it will maintain the 15-day fast-track closure process for low-risk cases under the new framework. You can use the 2025 88% 15-day closure rate as your starting point for 2028 compliance planning, and adjust your internal workflows to accommodate the new harmonized form requirements in the 19-month transition window.

**My planned FDI is in the manufacturing sector. What is the actual risk of receiving a formal Commission opinion?**

That rate applies across all sectors, including manufacturing. The 12% share of 2025 cases that required an in-depth security risk assessment does not translate directly to a formal opinion, as most in-depth reviews resolve with no formal objection once the investor provides additional requested documentation.
