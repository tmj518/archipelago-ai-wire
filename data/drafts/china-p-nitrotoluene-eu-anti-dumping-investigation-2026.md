## What happened

The Ministry of Commerce of the People's Republic of China issued Announcement No. 44 of 2026 on 3 October 2026, opening an anti-dumping investigation into imports of p-nitrotoluene originating in the European Union. The announcement is the instrument. It sets the product scope, the periods, the registration window and the timetable.

The product is defined by one customs code: **29042020** under the Import and Export Tariff of the People's Republic of China. That is the line a Chinese importer declares when the goods arrive from an EU supplier. The code is the whole of the scope as published — there is no annex of sub-descriptions in the announcement.

The registration clause is the operative one for a buyer. The announcement states that interested parties shall, within 20 days of the date of publication, register with the Trade Remedy Investigation Bureau of the Ministry of Commerce to participate in the anti-dumping investigation. Twenty days from 3 October 2026 is the window. It is not a comment period and it is not a hearing; it is the act that puts a party on the record.

> **Interested party** — in a Chinese trade-remedy case, a party the investigation recognises as having a stake in the outcome: the foreign exporter, the Chinese importer, the domestic industry that petitioned, and their trade associations. Registration is how a party becomes one. A party that does not register does not receive the questionnaire, does not file a response, and is not heard on injury, causation or the margin.

The questionnaire timing matters because it is short. The announcement says the ministry normally issues questionnaires to interested parties within 10 working days of the registration deadline. A registered importer therefore has a fixed, published sequence: register inside 20 days, receive the questionnaire inside roughly the following two weeks, and answer on the schedule the questionnaire itself sets.

## Why now

The dates explain the timing better than any commentary. The dumping period closes on 30 June 2026, three months before the announcement. The injury period runs back to 1 January 2022, four and a half years of import and pricing data. A case built on those two windows is a case about what EU material did to the Chinese market over that stretch, and the announcement opens it once the data is complete.

The other timing fact is the end date. The investigation normally concludes by 3 October 2027, with a possible 6-month extension. That is a year from publication, and it is the horizon an importer plans against.

## So what

The decision in front of an importer is not whether duties will come. It is whether to register. Those are different questions with different costs, and only one of them is inside the importer's control.

Registration costs an administrative act. Not registering costs the ability to shape the outcome. An importer that buys EU p-nitrotoluene under 29042020 and does not register is a party the investigation does not hear. It does not get the questionnaire, so it does not put its purchase prices, its contract terms or its end-use on the record. When the final determination is written, the importer's own transaction data is not in it.

The arithmetic that follows is about exposure, not about a rate. No duty rate is published in Announcement No. 44 of 2026. The announcement opens the case; it does not price it. What an importer can do now is size the line it is exposed on:

Take the annual value of EU-origin 29042020 purchases and treat it as the base. That base is what a duty, if one is imposed at the end, would apply to. The announcement does not supply the rate, so the exposure is the base itself, undiscounted. An importer that spends the next twelve months buying EU material without a registered position is carrying that base with no voice in how it is treated.

There is a second cost, and it is the one that gets missed. The dumping period is already closed. Whatever an importer paid for EU p-nitrotoluene between 1 July 2025 and 30 June 2026 is data the investigation will use, whether or not the importer registers. Registration is the only route to explaining that data — to showing that a price was a contract price, a grade difference, a freight term, or a volume discount rather than a dumped price. An unregistered importer's invoices are read by the other side and not defended by the importer.

The practical sequence for a buyer is short. Confirm whether any purchase in the last fifteen months was EU-origin 29042020. If it was, register inside the 20-day window. Then prepare for the questionnaire inside the 10 working days that follow, because the answer window is set by the questionnaire and not by the importer's calendar.

For importers weighing a switch of origin, the same logic that governs [how to tell a factory from a trading company](/blog/how-to-tell-a-factory-from-a-trading-company/) applies here: the origin on the invoice is the fact that decides which regime the goods enter, and a relabelled consignment does not change the code. The documents that carry origin through the border are set out in [the import documents checklist](/blog/importing-from-china-documents-checklist/), and they are the same documents a trade-remedy questionnaire will ask for.

## FAQ

**Does this announcement impose a duty on EU p-nitrotoluene?**

No. Announcement No. 44 of 2026 opens an investigation; it does not set a duty rate, a provisional measure or a collection date. The instrument sets the product scope at tariff code 29042020, the dumping period at 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026, the injury period at 1 January 2022 to 30 June 2026, and the timetable. The normal end of the investigation is 3 October 2027, with a possible 6-month extension. A duty becomes a priced fact only when a determination publishes a rate. Until then, an importer's exposure is the value of its EU-origin 29042020 purchases, undiscounted.

**What happens if an importer does not register within the 20 days?**

The importer is not an interested party on the record. It does not receive the questionnaire that the ministry normally issues within 10 working days of the registration deadline, so it files no response and puts no purchase data, contract terms or end-use evidence before the investigation. Its invoices from the dumping period are still part of the record the case examines, because that period closed on 30 June 2026. The difference is that an unregistered importer's transactions are read without the importer's explanation attached to them.

**Which purchases fall inside the dumping period?**

Any EU-origin p-nitrotoluene declared under tariff code 29042020 with a transaction date from 1 July 2025 to 30 June 2026. That window is closed, so the data already exists in the importer's books and in the customs record. The injury period reaches further back, to 1 January 2022, and covers the same code. An importer that bought EU material in either window has data in the case. Registration is the step that lets the importer speak to that data rather than leave it to the other parties.

## The point

The announcement does not tell an importer what the duty will be. It tells the importer that a clock started on 3 October 2026 and that the clock is 20 days long. Everything an importer does after that — the questionnaire, the response, the argument about whether a price was dumped — depends on being on the record before the window closes. The cost of registering is an administrative act. The cost of not registering is a year of exposure with no voice in it, on a tariff line the importer already buys.
