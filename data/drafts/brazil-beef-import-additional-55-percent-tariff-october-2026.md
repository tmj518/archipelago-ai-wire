## FAQ

**Can I avoid the 55% additional tariff if my Brazilian beef shipment was already on the water before 1 October 2026?**
No. The effective rule ties the additional tariff to the timestamp when customs accepts your import declaration, not to the date the vessel departed Brazil, the date the sales contract was signed, or the date the cargo arrives at the Chinese port. Even a vessel that docked on 30 September 2026 will pay the full 55% extra if its import declaration is not accepted before 00:00 on 1 October 2026. The only way to avoid the charge for in-transit cargo is to file and get full acceptance of your customs declaration before the deadline, which requires pre-clearance arrangements with your broker and all documentation fully validated in advance.

**Does the 55% additional tariff apply to Brazilian beef that is already stored in a Chinese bonded warehouse on 1 October 2026?**
Yes. The additional tariff applies when you submit the declaration to withdraw the goods from the bonded warehouse into domestic circulation. The date the goods entered the bonded warehouse does not reset the tariff rule that applies at the moment of final clearance. If you do not file the declaration for domestic entry before 00:00 on 1 October 2026, you pay the full 55% additional duty on the entire CIF value of the warehouse-stored shipment. You can file a declaration to move the goods between bonded warehouse locations after the deadline without triggering the additional tariff, but that only delays the cost to the moment you withdraw the goods for sale.

**Will this 55% additional tariff be removed once the next 2027 beef safeguard quota period opens?**
The published execution notice only covers the 2026 quota cycle, and no public guidance has been released for the 2027 quota allocation yet. The standard structure for China's annual agricultural safeguard measures resets the national quota volumes on 1 January of each year. The 55% additional tariff will remain in effect for all Brazilian beef shipments until the end of the 2026 quota year, unless an earlier official notice is published to suspend or cancel the measure. Importers should not assume the tariff will automatically reset on 1 January 2027, and should monitor official Ministry of Commerce announcements for 2027 quota rules before placing new long-term orders.
