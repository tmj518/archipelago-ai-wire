## What happened

The European Commission Directorate-General for Trade and Economic Security released its sixth annual FDI screening performance report on 14 October 2026, covering full 2025 operational data for the EU-wide FDI cooperation mechanism. The report confirms a 40% year-on-year increase in notifications from 477 in 2024 to 667 in 2025, the highest annual rise since the mechanism was first introduced.

Of the 667 total 2025 notifications, 88% were fully closed within 15 days of submission. 12% of notified investments triggered an in-depth security risk assessment, a 4 percentage point rise over 2024. Across the three consecutive reporting years 2023, 2024 and 2025, the European Commission issued formal opinions in less than 2% of all notified cases.

Sector breakdown data shows 27% of all 2025 notifications came from the manufacturing sector, followed by information and communication technologies at 19%, financial activities at 17%, wholesale and retail at 13%, and professional activities at 8%. The updated EU Foreign Investment Screening Regulation (FISR), adopted 17 June 2026, will apply in full from 17 January 2028.

> **EU FDI screening notification** — a mandatory or voluntary submission by a third-country investor to the relevant EU Member State authority and the European Commission cooperation mechanism, disclosing details of a foreign direct investment transaction that may impact EU security or public order. The notification triggers a standardized review process that runs on published timelines, with no hidden administrative delays for complete submissions.

## FAQ

**Do I need to file an FDI notification for every small share purchase in an EU-based import business?**
No. The 2025 performance data shows that less than 2% of notified cases trigger formal Commission opinions, and the vast majority of small, low-stakes share purchases in non-sensitive sectors fall under the 88% of cases that close within 15 days. You only need to file a notification if your transaction meets the national screening thresholds set by the specific EU Member State where the investment is taking place. Most Member States exempt small, passive shareholdings below 10% of total voting rights from mandatory notification requirements.

**How will the 17 January 2028 updated FISR change my 2026 notification workflows?**
The 19-month transition window between now and 17 January 2028 gives you ample time to adjust your internal processes. The updated regulation expands the scope of transactions that require notification to cover additional critical technology and supply chain segments, but the 2025 performance data shows the core 15-day fast-track closure process will remain in place for low-risk submissions. You can start mapping your existing investment portfolio against the new expanded scope rules in 2026 to avoid last-minute compliance gaps when the new rules take effect.

**What happens if my notification is selected for an in-depth security risk assessment?**
The 12% of 2025 cases that required in-depth assessment moved past the initial 15-day review window to a longer, structured evaluation process. No public data shows any of these cases resulted in a full transaction block for non-sensitive commercial investments, and the 2% formal opinion rate across three years confirms that EU-level intervention remains extremely rare. You will receive a formal written request for additional information from the relevant Member State authority, with a clear deadline for submission, before any extended review period begins.
