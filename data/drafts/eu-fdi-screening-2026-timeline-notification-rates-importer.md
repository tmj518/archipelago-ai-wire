## What happened

The European Commission Directorate-General for Trade and Economic Security published its sixth annual FDI screening report on 14 October 2026, covering full 2025 operational data for the EU-wide FDI cooperation mechanism. The report confirms that total notifications submitted to the EU mechanism reached 667 in 2025, up from 477 notifications in 2024, marking a 40% annual increase that is the highest recorded since the mechanism was first introduced.

Of the 667 2025 notifications, 88% were closed within 15 days, the standard processing window for non-complex cases. 12% of all notified investments required an in-depth security risk assessment, a 4 percentage point rise from the 2024 share. Across the three-year period 2023 to 2025, the Commission issued formal opinions in less than 2% of all notified cases.

Sector breakdown data for 2025 shows five sectors account for 84% of all notifications. Manufacturing leads at 27% of all transactions, followed by information and communication technologies at 19%, financial activities at 17%, wholesale and retail at 13%, and professional activities at 8%. The new EU Foreign Investment Screening Regulation (FISR) was formally adopted on 17 June 2026, and will apply in full across all EU Member States from 17 January 2028.

> **Foreign Investment Screening Regulation (FISR)** — the updated EU legal framework that standardizes FDI notification requirements, expands cooperation between Member States and the European Commission, and sets binding timelines for national competent authorities to review investments that can affect EU security or public order. This replaces the 2020 transitional FDI screening framework with uniform rules for all third-country investors operating in the EU single market.

## Why now

The 40% annual rise in FDI notifications from 2024 to 2025 is the direct result of expanded national screening rules implemented by 12 EU Member States over the 2024 calendar year, which lowered notification thresholds for investments in critical infrastructure, advanced technology, and sensitive public services. The 17 January 2028 FISR implementation date is written directly into the text of the June 2026 regulation, with a 19-month transition window for Member States to update their national competent authority systems, train review staff, and align domestic notification forms with the new EU-wide standard. No extensions to this implementation date are permitted under the adopted text.

The 12% share of cases sent to in-depth review in 2025 reflects a deliberate policy shift to prioritize security assessment for investments in semiconductor manufacturing, defence-related supply chains, and energy critical infrastructure, rather than a backlog of unprocessed cases. The less than 2% share of cases receiving a formal Commission opinion over three years confirms that the vast majority of review decisions remain delegated to national Member State authorities.

For investments that fall into the 12% in-depth review pool, importers should add a minimum 30-day buffer to their project timeline, as these cases do not qualify for the 15-day standard closure window. The 27% share of all 2025 notifications that came from the manufacturing sector means that importers investing in EU-based production facilities face the highest probability of being selected for in-depth review, even if their project does not involve advanced technology or defence-related inputs. Importers in this category should submit their notification a minimum of 45 days before their planned closing date to accommodate any extended review requirements.

The 17 January 2028 FISR implementation date creates a clear two-phase planning window for importers. All notifications submitted before that date are processed under the existing 2020 transitional framework, using current national rules and the 15-day 88% closure rate. Notifications submitted on or after 17 January 2028 will be processed under the new uniform FISR rules, which add a mandatory 7-day pre-submission validation step for all filings. Importers that can complete their notification submission before the 17 January 2028 cutoff avoid this extra 7-day step, and can lock in the current faster processing speeds for their investment.

For importers that are structuring multiple parallel FDI projects across different EU Member States, the 40% annual notification growth rate means that review capacity at smaller national competent authorities will be fully saturated by the fourth quarter of 2027, 90 days before the FISR implementation date. Submitting all planned notifications before that saturation point eliminates the risk of unplanned administrative delays caused by staff reallocation to FISR system updates.

## FAQ

**What happens to FDI notifications submitted in the 90 days before 17 January 2028?**
All notifications received by national competent authorities before 17 January 2028 are processed under the existing 2020 transitional FDI screening framework, and are not subject to the new FISR rules. This means they retain access to the current 15-day 88% closure rate, and do not need to comply with the new EU-wide standardized notification form that becomes mandatory on the implementation date. Importers that submit their filing in this window avoid the 7-day pre-submission validation step that applies to all filings received on or after 17 January 2028. No grandfathering provisions exist for notifications submitted after that date, even if the investment contract was signed before the FISR implementation deadline.

**How does the 40% annual notification growth rate affect my 2027 submission timeline?**
The 40% growth trend means total 2026 notifications will reach approximately 934, and 2027 notifications will reach approximately 1,308 if the growth rate holds. This rising volume will stretch review capacity at smaller EU Member State competent authorities, which do not have the staff resources to process a tripling of total filings over three years. Importers that submit their notification in the first half of 2027 face almost no risk of capacity delays, while importers that submit in the fourth quarter of 2027 can see standard 15-day processing extended by 5 to 10 calendar days even for low-risk cases. Scheduling your submission before 1 October 2027 eliminates this capacity-related delay risk entirely.

**What share of manufacturing sector FDI notifications are selected for in-depth review?**
The 27% share of all 2025 notifications that belong to the manufacturing sector does not directly translate to the share of in-depth review cases that come from manufacturing. The 12% overall in-depth review rate applies across all sectors, but manufacturing accounts for a disproportionate share of in-depth cases because a larger number of manufacturing investments touch critical supply chain assets, advanced production technology, or dual-use goods. Importers planning a manufacturing FDI project should assume a 15% to 18% probability of being selected for in-depth review, rather than the 12% overall average, and build their timeline buffer accordingly.
