The EU FDI screening rules apply from **17 January 2028**. That date gives businesses a fixed compliance deadline, but the caseload is already growing.

A company preparing for 17 January 2028 should therefore not treat screening as a low-volume formality. The mechanism has already been handling a larger workload, and the published outcomes show that a measurable minority of notifications moves beyond the 15-calendar-day track.

## FAQ

**Will every notification close within 15 calendar days?**

No. The Commission reports that **88%** of notified cases closed within **15 calendar days**, which is a strong majority but not a guarantee. On the reported total of **667 notifications**, that equals roughly 587 cases. The other 12% did not follow that fast-track outcome. A transaction timetable that assumes closure in 15 calendar days for every filing ignores the reported minority and leaves no room for additional information requests or security analysis.

**How likely is an in-depth security risk assessment?**

The reported rate is **12%** of notified investments. Applied to 667 notifications, that is approximately **80 cases**. In plain planning terms, a business should treat deeper review as roughly a one-in-eight possibility for each notified investment. The actual result for an individual transaction will be binary—either it enters the assessment or it does not—but the 12% rate is the best portfolio-level expectation supported by the published figures.

**Why does manufacturing matter?**

Manufacturing matters because it accounts for **53% of in-depth assessment cases**, more than half of the deeper-review group. Using the approximate total of 80 deeper cases, 53% corresponds to roughly **42 manufacturing cases**. A manufacturing investor should not assume review is automatic, but it should expect greater scrutiny concentration than businesses in sectors that make up smaller shares of the in-depth caseload. Ownership, technology, inputs, and EU production footprint should be documented early.

**Does a low opinion rate mean low compliance risk?**

No. The Commission issued opinions in **less than 2%** of cases, but **12%** required an in-depth security risk assessment. Those are different outcomes. A transaction can incur delay, management attention, document production, and uncertainty without becoming one of the fewer-than-2% of cases in which an opinion is issued. The official figures do not provide a monetary cost, but they do show that deeper scrutiny is several times more common than an opinion.
