## What happened

On 18 September 2026, the European Commission Directorate-General for Trade and Economic Security announced that the Commission was imposing provisional safeguard measures on imports of grain-oriented electrical steel. The safeguard investigation opened in March 2026. The chosen regime combines tariff-rate quotas with price thresholds.

The EU grain-oriented electrical steel provisional safeguard TRQ price threshold 2026 announcement does not publish a quota volume, a threshold price, an out-of-quota charge, or a declaration deadline. It does state the procedural and voting figures that govern the rest of the case. Importers must use the announcement for scope and timing, not for a per-unit duty number.

> **Tariff-rate quota and price threshold** — A tariff-rate quota admits a set volume under one treatment and applies a separate treatment beyond that volume. A price threshold compares the declared import price with a specified level. The announcement names both controls but withholds the controlling figures.

## Why now

The September announcement arrives while the investigation is open. A safeguard investigation normally completes within nine months; in exceptional circumstances the period extends to 11 months. The March 2026 launch therefore sets a bounded decision window, but the supplied text gives no final-decision date.

Definitive measures are not automatic. When the Commission proposes them, at least 15 Member States representing 65% of the EU population must vote in favour. The enforcement backdrop is active: the Commission imposed 26 definitive trade defence measures in 2025, had 232 measures in place at year end, and applied measures retroactively in three cases. Those figures explain why importers need dated records, but they are not GOES duty rates.

## So what

This is a directional trade-policy event. The announcement changes planning and contract risk, but it supplies no numeric border charge. The cost arithmetic stops at three missing inputs.

Let **Q** be the quota volume, **P** be the threshold price, **C** be the charge after the quota is exhausted or the price falls below the threshold, **V** be shipment quantity, and **U** be declared unit price. The announcement gives no Q, P, or C. Without Q, cumulative volume cannot be tested against the quota. Without P, U cannot be tested against the threshold. Without C, no charge can be multiplied by V. The per-unit result is not calculable.

For GOES coils, the quota test compares cumulative covered imports with Q, and the price test compares U with P. If the shipment remains within Q and U is at or above P, the two safeguard tests create no additional charge under the stated design. If volume exceeds Q or U is below P, the instrument's charge applies. Both outcomes remain open because Q and P are absent.

For laminations and cores, classification comes first. A lamination cut from GOES and a core assembled from those laminations each require a scope determination. If the item is outside the product scope, the GOES safeguard does not apply. If it is covered, the same Q, P, and C tests apply. Supplier statements on GOES weight, origin, and processing stage are therefore cost documents, not only quality records.

For transformers, the level of incorporation matters. A core already built into a transformer either falls under a finished-goods classification or remains separately identified by the product scope. The supplied announcement gives no numeric threshold or code annex that resolves that choice. Importers cannot derive a GOES charge from the transformer's total value without a published rule.

The usable arithmetic is calendar arithmetic. Importers have a normal nine-month review period and an outer 11-month limit. They can classify goods, collect evidence, amend contract clauses, and schedule sourcing reviews inside that window. Any euro, dollar, or per-tonne duty figure added now comes from outside the supplied record and has no support.

## FAQ

**Are GOES laminations, cores, and transformers containing those cores covered?**

The announcement identifies grain-oriented electrical steel as the product under safeguard. Importers cannot resolve coverage from the material name alone. Laminations cut from GOES, cores assembled from those laminations, and cores already incorporated in transformers each require a line-by-line check against the product scope and applicable tariff classification. Keep supplier specifications, GOES net weight, processing records, and product drawings. A finished transformer and a separate core do not automatically share the same treatment. Treat scope as the first compliance step; only after coverage is settled do quota and price-threshold rules enter the landed-cost calculation.

**What extra duty applies per tonne under the provisional regime?**

No per-tonne safeguard amount can be calculated from the supplied announcement. A tariff-rate quota requires a quota volume and an out-of-quota charge; a price-threshold regime requires the threshold price and the rule for valuing shipments below it. The announcement names both mechanisms but publishes none of those numeric inputs. The correct arithmetic is therefore conditional: covered goods inside the quota and at or above the threshold follow one treatment, while goods outside the quota or below the threshold follow the instrument's charge. Importers should reject any landed-cost sheet that inserts an assumed rate. A numeric article becomes possible only when the regulation supplies the missing values.

**When do provisional measures become definitive?**

The safeguard investigation launched in March 2026 operates within a normal nine-month period. In exceptional circumstances the period extends to 11 months. When the Commission proposes definitive safeguard measures, adoption requires support from at least 15 EU Member States representing 65% of the EU population. The announcement does not state a specific final-decision date, and provisional action does not guarantee definitive action. Importers should use the nine-to-eleven-month window to review contracts, classify downstream products, and assemble evidence. The vote thresholds determine whether the provisional regime converts into a longer-lived requirement or ends without definitive measures.
