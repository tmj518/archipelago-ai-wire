> **Definitive anti-dumping duty** — a final, legally binding percentage-based import tax imposed by the European Commission on goods found to be dumped into the EU market at prices below their normal value in the country of origin. Unlike provisional duties, definitive duties apply for a fixed multi-year term, and no subsequent refund or adjustment is available outside of a formal interim review or sunset review process.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between the provisional anti-dumping duty and the new definitive duty for Chinese pea protein?**
Provisional duties applied from 29 April 2026 to 24 September 2026, as a temporary measure while the full investigation was completed. Those provisional rates were subject to later adjustment, with importers able to claim partial refunds if the final definitive rates came in lower than the provisional amounts they paid. The new definitive duties that took effect 25 September 2026 are final, fixed, and apply for a full five-year term. No refunds or downward adjustments are available for the duration of the term, outside of a formal interim review application submitted by an interested party and approved by the European Commission. All definitive duty rates are legally binding and non-negotiable for all importers that do not qualify for a specific exemption.

**How do I confirm which specific duty rate (40.5% or 67.1%) applies to my Chinese pea protein shipment?**
The official European Commission notice that published the definitive duty measures includes an explicit list of named Chinese exporting producers that have been assigned the lower 40.5% individual duty rate. All other Chinese exporters not named on that list, and all importers that cannot demonstrate their shipment was manufactured by one of the named qualified producers, will be charged the standard 67.1% residual duty rate. You must hold a valid, verifiable certificate of origin and supporting documentation that links your shipment directly to one of the named low-duty producers to qualify for the 40.5% rate. If you cannot produce that documentation at the time of customs declaration, EU customs authorities will automatically apply the higher 67.1% rate.

**Can I shift my pea protein sourcing to non-Chinese origins to avoid these duties entirely?**
Yes, the definitive anti-dumping duties apply only to pea protein that originates in China. Pea protein sourced from EU domestic producers, or from third countries outside of China, is not subject to these new anti-dumping charges. Importers that shift sourcing to alternative origins will not pay any of the 40.5% to 67.1% duty, though they may face higher base FOB pricing from non-Chinese suppliers. You should verify the full origin chain for any new supplier you work with, to ensure no part of the raw pea cultivation, protein extraction, or processing steps took place in China, which could still trigger the anti-dumping duty at EU customs.
