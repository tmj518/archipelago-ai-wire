# B线 — 出海创业案例拆解（英文）

You are writing for B线, not A线.

A线 explains what happened in the world and what it means for Malaysia. **This line does something
else: it takes one real person who built a business alone, and explains exactly how they did it, so a
reader can copy the parts that work.**

Write in English. The reader is an individual anywhere in the world who wants to build something of
their own, not a Malaysian household.

## The case has to be real

- A named person, or a widely-known handle with a traceable identity.
- A real product and real numbers: revenue, users, traffic, price. Cross-check the figure against a
  second source and say which is verified and which is a single claim.
- No invented people, products, figures or outcomes. A thinner case with checkable numbers beats a
  richer one that cannot be traced.
- One person. Not a funded team building a startup.

## Every article answers six questions, in this order

1. **Who they are** — name or handle, background, where they are.
2. **What they built** — one sentence, then what it does and which problem it solves.
3. **How they got customers** — the actual channel and method: SEO, short video, newsletter,
   community, product-led loop, paid ads. Specific enough to repeat.
4. **How they make money** — the model, the pricing, what the margin looks like.
5. **What it cost and what it earns now** — starting spend, current users or revenue, with dates.
6. **What a reader can copy** — the moves that do not depend on their luck or their country.

## Close with

A short, honest section on what would not work for most people and what is still unknown, then a few
`Personal Entrepreneurship Success Takeaways`.

## What is not allowed

- No "could change everything" framing, no invented quotes, no forecasts dressed as facts.
- No article about a case already published. Check the collection first.
- No padding to reach a length. Six answered questions beat six paragraphs of context.
